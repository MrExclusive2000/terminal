## Executive summary

Research date 2026-08-25. All prices/limits verified live; "as of" dates given per finding.

The single-user constraint changes the data-engineering shape more than it changes the compliance shape. Two consequences dominate. (1) Scarcity is quota and bandwidth, not dollars: Massive/Polygon Stocks Advanced is $199/mo with unlimited calls, EODHD is 100,000 weighted calls/day at 1,000 req/min, Tiingo Power is $30/mo but capped at 40 GB/month of bandwidth. So the router must optimise weighted-quota-remaining, not price. (2) The market-data licence collapses to one subscriber fee — but the pro/non-pro fork is real and expensive: Massive's $199 real-time Stocks Advanced tier is explicitly "Individual use, Non-pros only", so trading through an LLC ejects the user from that plan entirely. Databento (Standard $199/mo, live included, self-service professional questionnaire) is the pro-column answer.

Three areas are where a naive plan silently produces wrong numbers. First, corporate actions: store RAW bars only plus a separate factor table and adjust at query time; storing adjusted bars means every split rewrites years of files, and the classic double-adjust bug (Massive's aggregates endpoint defaults to adjusted=true) is invisible until you compare against another terminal. Second, trade-condition eligibility: average-price trades (CTA cond B / UTP cond W) update volume but not OHLC; odd lots and Form-T prints have their own rules; drive the bar builder off the vendor's conditions reference endpoint, not a hardcoded switch. Third, time: the FX day boundary is 17:00 wall-clock America/New_York, so its UTC instant moves twice a year; session-date must be a stored column, never derived from ToLocalTime().Date.

For the insiders pillar the highest-leverage, least-exploited facts are: EDGAR's browse-edgar?action=getcurrent Atom feed gives seconds-level Form 4 detection well inside the 10 req/s fair-access budget; Form 144 has been structured XML on EDGAR since 2023-04-13 and is filed BEFORE the sale, giving 1–3 days' lead on the Form 4 that confirms it; reportingOwner CIK is a free, stable, global person key that builds the cross-company director graph; and footnotes — not fields — carry the 10b5-1 plan adoption date, which is precisely where an LLM extraction layer earns its cost.

For the AI layer: Anthropic ships no embedding model — pair Claude (Opus 5 $5/$25 per MTok) with Voyage (voyage-3.5 from $0.06/M, 200M free tokens on the voyage-4 family) and run authoring through the Batch API at 50% cost. Knowledge packs must be signed, data-only, schema-validated artefacts; the pack channel and NuGet are the two real supply-chain adversaries.

### 2. SEC EDGAR fair access: hard 10 req/s, mandatory User-Agent, 403 + ~10-minute IP block on violation; EFTS is throttled harder than the archives  
`confidence: high`

Policy in force since 2021-07-27 and confirmed current on sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data as of 2026-08-25: maximum 10 requests/second aggregate across all machines you control. Every request must carry a User-Agent identifying company/name and contact email; a missing or default library UA returns 403 Forbidden and blocks the source IP for approximately 10 minutes. data.sec.gov requires no API key and does not support CORS. Update latency stated by the SEC: submissions API sub-second after dissemination; XBRL companyconcept/companyfacts/frames under one minute, longer at peak filing times. Daily/full indexes are compiled nightly starting ~22:00 ET and 'usually complete within a few hours'. EDGAR transmission hours are 06:00–22:00 ET on business days; a live submission beginning after 17:30 ET receives the NEXT business day's filing date (06:00 ET) and is not disseminated until then. The full-text search endpoint (efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=...) is undocumented — no published parameter list, response schema, rate figure or deprecation policy — covers filing body text from 2001 forward, and is reported to throttle more aggressively than the document servers.

**Why it matters.** The governor for SEC must fail CLOSED, not best-effort: one burst costs a 10-minute blackout during exactly the window (market open, 16:05 ET Form 4 dumps) when the terminal's differentiator is live. Budget the 10 req/s explicitly: reserve ~3 req/s for the real-time Form 4 poll, ~3 for backfill, ~1 for EFTS (treat as 1–2 req/s ceiling), leave headroom. The 17:30 ET cutoff means an 'insider filed today' badge must key on periodOfReport and acceptedAt, not on filed date, or you will mislabel every late-afternoon filing.

### 3. Near-real-time Form 4 detection: browse-edgar?action=getcurrent (Atom) is the only free sub-minute discovery channel — but it has a rolling window, so it MUST be paired with a reconciling sweep  
`confidence: high`

Discovery options ranked by latency: (1) https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcurrent&type=4&owner=include&count=100&output=atom — reflects filings within seconds-to-minutes of acceptance; commercial Form 4 services state they parse and webhook 'within a minute or two' off this same feed. (2) data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK##########.json — SEC states sub-second delay, but it is PER-CIK, so it is a follow-up/verification channel for watchlist names, not a discovery channel. (3) Daily index (/Archives/edgar/daily-index/YYYY/QTRn/) — compiled nightly from ~22:00 ET; complete but useless for intraday. (4) EFTS full-text — indexing lag plus stricter throttling; use for retrospective research only. The getcurrent feed returns only a rolling recent window (last N entries / few hours), so any outage longer than that window loses filings permanently from that channel.

**Why it matters.** The differentiating claim 'you see the insider print before the crowd' rests entirely on this. Design: poll getcurrent every 2–5s during 06:00–22:00 ET (well within budget), dedupe by accession number, then run a mandatory reconciliation sweep at ~22:30 ET against the daily index plus a per-CIK submissions sweep for every watchlist company. Belt-and-braces is not optional here — a laptop that sleeps for four hours will otherwise silently drop the afternoon's filings and the user will never know what he missed.

### 4. Form 345 ownershipDocument XML: the signal is in the footnotes and the transaction code, not in the headline share count  
`confidence: high`

Root element ownershipDocument. Two parallel tables: nonDerivativeTable (nonDerivativeTransaction / nonDerivativeHolding) and derivativeTable (derivativeTransaction / derivativeHolding). Every numeric is wrapped as <transactionShares><value>N</value><footnoteId id="F1"/></transactionShares> — footnoteId is a SIBLING of value, so a naive //value XPath discards the footnote linkage entirely. Transaction codes (transactionCoding/transactionCode) and their signal content: P = open-market/private purchase (high signal), S = sale (high signal), A = grant/award (compensation, near-zero directional signal), F = shares withheld to pay exercise price or tax (mechanical, zero signal), M = exercise/conversion of derivative, X = exercise of an in-the-money option, C = conversion of derivative, D = disposition back to issuer, G = bona fide gift, J = other (nature must be described in text), K = equity swap. Roughly half the raw Form 4 universe is A/F/M/X mechanical activity. The Rule 10b5-1(c) checkbox was added to Forms 4 and 5 in EDGAR release of 2023-03-20, mandatory for filings on/after 2023-04-01; the plan's ADOPTION DATE is required in the free-text 'Explanation of Responses' (i.e. the footnotes), not in a structured field. The checkbox must NOT be ticked for plans adopted before the amendments' effective date, so absence of the flag does not prove absence of a plan for grandfathered plans.

**Why it matters.** Three concrete pipeline requirements fall out. (a) Parse footnotes as first-class entities joined to the specific value they annotate — this is where 'sold to cover taxes', 'held by family trust', 'gift to donor-advised fund' and the 10b5-1 adoption date live. (b) Classify by transaction code before any aggregation; an 'insider bought $4m' headline built from code A grants is a lie. (c) The 10b5-1 adoption-date extraction from free text is an LLM job, and it is the single highest-value use of the AI layer in this pillar: 'plan adopted 41 days before the first sale' is the pattern the SEC's own cooling-off rule was written to address.

### 5. Form 144 is filed BEFORE the sale and has been structured XML since 2023-04-13 — a genuine 1–3 day lead indicator almost nobody ingests  
`confidence: high`

Form 144 ('Notice of Proposed Sale of Securities Pursuant to Rule 144') is required before a proposed sale exceeding 5,000 restricted shares or $50,000 aggregate value by an officer, director, 10% holder, or affiliated family. Electronic EDGAR filing became mandatory 2023-04-13 (SEC compliance-date announcement); the fillable XML form shipped 2022-09-27 and standalone Form 144 XML technical specifications were published 2022-10-17. Filing hours for Form 144 were separately extended by SEC final rule 33-11159 (2023). By contrast Form 4 is due two business days AFTER the transaction. So the sequence for a large affiliate sale is: Form 144 (intent, T-1 to T-3) → execution → Form 4 (confirmation, T+2).

**Why it matters.** This is the cheapest genuine edge in the entire insiders pillar and it is free. Joining 144 → 4 lets the terminal show 'notice filed, sale not yet confirmed' as a distinct state, flag 144s that never produce a matching Form 4 (plan cancelled — itself informative), and measure the delta between proposed and executed size. It also means the ingestion pipeline needs a stateful matcher across two form types keyed on (issuer CIK, reporting person, security class, approximate size, date window) — not a per-form ETL.

### 6. Ownership-side reference data got dramatically more machine-readable in 2024 — Schedules 13D/G are now structured XML, and deadlines collapsed  
`confidence: high`

Per the SEC's Oct-2023 beneficial-ownership final rules: initial Schedule 13D deadline shortened from 10 calendar days to 5 BUSINESS days; 13D amendments due within 2 business days. Effective 2024-09-30 for 13G: passive investors file initially within 5 business days of crossing 5%, amend within 2 business days of crossing 10% and on each subsequent 5% move; Qualified Institutional Investors and Exempt Investors file initially within 45 calendar days after the END OF THE CALENDAR QUARTER in which they cross 5%, amend within 5 business days after month-end on crossing 10%. Schedules 13D and 13G (excluding exhibits) must be filed in machine-readable structured XML; voluntary compliance opened 2023-12-18, mandatory from 2024-12-18. Form 13F remains: 45 calendar days after quarter end (deadlines land ~Feb 15 / May 15 / Aug 15 / Nov 15), cover page + summary page + information table all in XML (EDGAR Form 13F XML technical spec v1.7, EDGAR release 22.4.1, 2023-01-03); confidential treatment requests are also filed electronically.

**Why it matters.** Two design consequences. (1) 'Who owns it' history is asymmetric: post-2024-12-18 13D/G is a clean XML parse; pre-2024 it is HTML scraping with all the fragility that implies. Budget for a hybrid parser and be explicit in the UI about which era a datapoint came from. (2) The 13G quarterly-then-45-day path for QIIs means BlackRock/Vanguard-class stakes surface on a schedule, whereas activist 13Ds now surface in 5 business days — the alerting model must treat the two as different products with different urgency, not one 'ownership change' feed.

### 7. 'Who is paid to make the number' is machine-readable for the first time: Item 402(v) Pay-versus-Performance is Inline-XBRL tagged  
`confidence: high`

Reg S-K Item 402(v), adopted under Dodd-Frank §953(a), effective 2022-10-11, first applying to proxies for fiscal years ending on or after 2022-12-16. Registrants must tag the pay-versus-performance disclosure in Inline XBRL: each value in the PvP table separately tagged, footnote and relationship disclosures block-text tagged, and specified data within footnotes tagged. 'Compensation Actually Paid' (CAP) is a mark-to-market construct — it revalues unvested and outstanding equity awards each year and therefore differs materially from Summary Compensation Table totals and from cash received.

**Why it matters.** The rest of the proxy compensation disclosure (Summary Compensation Table, grant tables, performance-metric targets) is NOT XBRL-tagged and requires HTML/LLM extraction. PvP is the one structured beachhead. Pipeline consequence: PvP goes into the same XBRL fact store as the financials (same extraction path, near-zero marginal cost), and gives you a free CAP-vs-TSR series per issuer. Everything else in the incentives pillar — vesting cliffs, the specific EPS/FCF target a CEO's PSUs key off, hedging-policy language, clawback triggers (Exhibit 97 under Rule 10D-1) — is unstructured text and is the second core job for the AI layer.

### 8. Trade condition codes decide OHLC: get them wrong and your chart silently disagrees with every other terminal on the desk  
`confidence: high`

Under CTA and UTP plan specifications, each trade's Sale Conditions determine independently whether it may update Open, High/Low, Last, and Volume, and separately at Consolidated vs Market-Centre level. The governing rule is conjunctive-negative: if ANY one of a trade's sale conditions says NO for a given field, the trade is excluded from that field — the NO takes precedence. CTA uses four eligibility notes (Note 1: opening trade updates only if first qualifying trade; Note 3: late-reported trades update only if from the listing market or the same participant as the previous trade; Note 4: open-price update on first qualifying trade). Worked example from the vendor documentation: Average Price Trade (CTA condition B, UTP condition W) UPDATES VOLUME but does NOT update high/low or open/close. Massive/Polygon expose the full mapping via a conditions-and-indicators reference endpoint rather than requiring hardcoding.

**Why it matters.** This is the most common source of 'my minute bars don't match' and it is invisible without a reference implementation to compare against. Build the bar builder as a table-driven engine loaded from the vendor's conditions reference at startup and cached daily — never a switch statement in C#. Also: the tape emits explicit trade cancel and correction messages, so a minute bar is NOT immutable when the minute closes. Hold the trailing N bars mutable (recommend 3–5 minutes) and emit correction events downstream. And the official daily open/close from the listing exchange's auction is a different number from max/min of your minute bars — store and label both.

### 9. Time traps, enumerated — and the one that bites hardest is that the FX day boundary is a WALL-CLOCK rule whose UTC instant moves twice a year  
`confidence: high`

(1) FX week runs Sunday 17:00 to Friday 17:00 America/New_York, year-round — that is 21:00 UTC under EDT and 22:00 UTC under EST. Hardcoding 22:00 UTC produces wrong daily FX bars for roughly eight months of every year. (2) The four major centres change clocks on different dates: US second Sunday March / first Sunday November; UK and EU last Sunday March / last Sunday October; Sydney observes DST inverted (roughly first Sunday October to first Sunday April); Tokyo has no DST at all. Result: London–New York offset is 4h instead of 5h for about three weeks each spring and autumn, and overlap windows change length. (3) 'What is today for a Tokyo name': JST = UTC+9, no DST, so the 2026-08-25 Tokyo session spans 2026-08-24T23:00Z to 2026-08-25T06:00Z — a New York user at 21:00 ET on the 24th is already looking at the 25th's session. (4) Exchange lunch breaks: XTKS and HKEX have midday breaks; a naive minute-bar generator emits phantom empty bars through them. (5) US half-days (NYSE 13:00 ET early close: day after Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve when a weekday, July 3 in some years) break any fixed 390-bars-per-day validator. (6) tzdb churn is real and political — Mexico abolished most DST in 2022, Iran abolished DST in 2022, Egypt reinstated it in 2023 — so the zone database must be a shippable, versioned data pack, not a dependency on Windows Update. Windows registry time zones and IANA tzdb also disagree in edge cases.

**Why it matters.** Store every timestamp as a UTC instant PLUS two explicit columns: exchange MIC and session_date (the exchange's own business date), plus a session tag (PRE / RTH / POST / AUCTION / BREAK / HALT). Never derive session_date via ToLocalTime().Date. Use NodaTime with a pinned, app-shipped tzdb rather than System.TimeZoneInfo. There is no mature .NET equivalent of Python's exchange_calendars / pandas_market_calendars — the practical route is to generate calendar data (sessions, early closes, breaks) from those Python libraries offline and ship it as a versioned calendar pack the app validates against, with a manual override path for the inevitable ad-hoc exchange closure.

### 10. Symbology: the SEC's company_tickers.json has NO history, so a CIK↔ticker map built from it is quietly wrong for every renamed, delisted or reused symbol  
`confidence: high`

SEC bulk files: company_tickers.json (ticker/CIK/name), company_tickers_exchange.json (adds exchange), company_tickers_mf.json (fund CIK/series/class/ticker). All are CURRENT-STATE snapshots only — a delisted or renamed issuer simply disappears. OpenFIGI (free, no cost, openfigi.com/api) rate limits as of 2026-08-25: /v3/mapping without API key 25 requests/minute at 10 jobs/request (250 mappings/min); WITH a free API key 25 requests per 6 SECONDS at 100 jobs/request (up to 25,000 mappings/min) — a 100× throughput difference for a free key. /v3/search and /v3/filter: 5 req/min unauthenticated, 20 req/min with key. 22+ idType values including ID_ISIN, ID_CUSIP, ID_SEDOL, TICKER, BASE_TICKER, ID_EXCH_SYMBOL. FIGI has three levels — exchange-level, share-class-level, and composite (country-level) — and ownership/insider data joins at share-class level, not exchange level. Ticker reuse after delisting is routine (Enron delisted from NYSE Jan 2002, continued OTC as ENRNQ until Nov 2004). Vendor symbol conventions for class shares diverge: SEC 'BRK-B', Nasdaq/Massive 'BRK.B', Bloomberg 'BRK/B', Reuters 'BRKb'.

**Why it matters.** Design the symbology store as BITEMPORAL: (figi, ticker, cik, mic, currency, valid_from, valid_to, source, observed_at). Snapshot company_tickers.json daily and diff it — the diff IS your rename/delist event stream, and it is free. Key all internal storage on share-class FIGI, never on ticker. Get the free OpenFIGI key on day one. Note also that 13F information tables are CUSIP-keyed; CUSIP↔ticker mapping is commercially licensed, but OpenFIGI maps ID_CUSIP → FIGI → ticker for free, which is the legitimate route for a single non-redistributing user.

### 11. The double-adjust bug is structural, not a coding slip — and the fix is an architectural rule: persist RAW only, adjust at query time  
`confidence: high`

CRSP's methodology is the reference: returns (RET) are fully adjusted but PRICES are not; you divide by CFACPR (Cumulative Factor to Adjust Price). Adjustment is backward-ratio: the most recent day anchors at factor 1.0 and factors accumulate backwards. Dividend adjustment uses the ratio method — multiply pre-ex-date prices by (1 − D/P), specifically to avoid negative historical prices. Vendor APIs default to adjusted output (Massive/Polygon aggregates take adjusted=true by default), so a pipeline that fetches adjusted data and then applies its own split factors squares the adjustment. Volume adjusts in the OPPOSITE direction to price and many vendors either omit volume adjustment or apply it inconsistently. Additional traps: spin-offs need the spin ratio plus a when-issued price; special dividends are treated as regular by some vendors; reverse splits create fractional-share rounding differences.

**Why it matters.** Three hard rules. (1) The bar store contains RAW prices and RAW volume only; a separate corporate_actions table holds (figi, ex_date, type, ratio, cash_amount, source, announced_at); adjustment happens in the query layer. A newly announced split then invalidates a handful of factor rows, not years of Parquet. (2) Every series returned to the UI carries an explicit adjustment mode enum (RAW / SPLIT_ADJ / TOTAL_RETURN) and the chart labels it — because 'why does my 2019 entry price not match the chart' is the number one support question a terminal generates, even for an audience of one. (3) Form 4 share counts are as-filed and are NOT retroactively split-adjusted; comparing a 2019 filing's '10,000 shares' against a post-split 2026 price is a silent, plausible-looking error. Insider quantities must be adjusted through the same factor table, keyed on the filing's period date.

### 13. Streaming: gap detection has no universal mechanism — Databento offers exact intraday replay, most retail feeds offer nothing, so you need a time-based reconciler  
`confidence: medium`

Databento's Live API documents an intraday replay facility and states that the live API uses the same interfaces and data structures as historical replay, with multiplexed IP transit and redundant networks claimed to deliver 'virtually zero gaps on billions of messages per day' and 99.9+% uptime. Massive/Polygon's WebSocket clusters (stocks, forex, etc. are separate connections) require auth then subscribe with channel.symbol patterns (T.AAPL trades, Q.AAPL quotes, AM.AAPL minute aggregates), and individual plans cap concurrent connections per cluster — a reconnect issued before the old socket is reaped is rejected. Alpaca free tier is 1 concurrent connection, 30 trade/quote channels.

**Why it matters.** Concrete design. (a) Because most feeds carry no per-stream sequence number, gap detection must be temporal: maintain per-symbol expected-next-bar deadlines from the exchange calendar, and if a minute bar has not arrived within N seconds of the minute close, queue a REST backfill for that (symbol, minute) — then reconcile, because vendors also REVISE aggregates after publication. (b) Reconnect must be: close cleanly and wait for server-side reap, exponential backoff with full jitter, then a PRIORITISED resubscribe — focused panel first, then visible watchlist, then background — batched and staggered by 50–100 ms, never 2,000 individual subscribe frames. (c) On every reconnect, run a mandatory backfill window covering (last_message_ts − safety_margin) to now, and mark all bars in that window as reconciled. (d) If provable no-gap matters more than $ (it does for a bar store you will later trust), Databento's replay-from-timestamp is the strongest technical argument in the vendor comparison.

### 14. UI backpressure: split the stream into a LOSSLESS lane and a LOSSY lane — this is the single most important streaming design decision  
`confidence: high`

WPF/WinUI reality: binding a collection to a tick stream raises CollectionChanged on the dispatcher per item, so 5,000 ticks/sec is 5,000 layout/measure passes per second and the UI thread dies. Established mitigations from the WPF charting ecosystem: throttle rendering to a MaxFrameRate-driven DispatcherTimer (default ~60 FPS); lower render priority BELOW mouse/input priority so the message loop is not starved; CompositionTarget.Rendering fires ~60 FPS while updates are occurring and throttles to ~50 FPS when idle; conflate multiple updates before handing them to the consumer whenever the consumer is slower than the producer.

**Why it matters.** Two lanes with different guarantees, both built on System.Threading.Channels. LOSSY lane (quotes, last-price ticks feeding price cells, depth): a bounded channel with BoundedChannelFullMode.DropOldest feeding a per-symbol latest-wins dictionary that the UI drains on a 60–100 ms timer; dropping is CORRECT here because only the latest quote matters. LOSSLESS lane (trades feeding the bar builder, and every EDGAR filing event): an unbounded or bounded-with-wait channel — dropping a trade corrupts the bar, dropping a Form 4 defeats the product. Conflate only in the lossy lane, and never conflate anything that is an input to persisted state. Additionally: charts must render to a WriteableBitmap / custom DrawingVisual, not to thousands of framework elements, and grids must use UI virtualisation with recycling.

### 15. Storage sizing: 5y of 1-minute bars on 3,000 US tickers is roughly 12–25 GB in Parquet+zstd; full tick archives are ~1.35 GB PER DAY and must not be archived  
`confidence: medium`

Arithmetic (medium confidence, stated assumptions). Sessions: ~252/yr × 5 = 1,260. RTH minutes 390/day (09:30–16:00 ET); extended 04:00–20:00 = 960/day. Dense RTH upper bound: 3,000 × 390 × 1,260 = 1.474 billion rows; realistically 50–70% of that because minutes with no trades are omitted for the long tail → ~0.9–1.1bn rows RTH-only, ~1.4–1.8bn including pre/post. Massive/Polygon minute-aggregate flat-file schema is 8 columns (ticker, volume, open, close, high, low, window_start in Unix nanoseconds, transactions), updated 11:00 ET for the prior day. Anchor: a single day's SIP TRADES flat file (us_stocks_sip/trades_v1/2024/04/2024-04-05.csv.gz) is approximately 1.35 GB gzipped; Massive's total flat-file corpus across all data types is stated around 12 TB. Estimated footprints: minute bars for 3,000 tickers over 5y — ~40–80 GB as CSV.gz, ~12–25 GB as Parquet with zstd and delta-encoded timestamps/prices, ~20–35 GB in a DuckDB native file. Daily bars 3,000 × 20 years ≈ 15m rows, under 200 MB. FX 30 pairs × 1-minute × 5y ≈ 56m rows, ~0.5–0.9 GB. Live tick ring buffer for a 200-symbol watchlist on a busy day ≈ 3–6m trades ≈ 100–200 MB resident at ~24–32 bytes per packed struct. US ADV context for scale: 19.7bn shares/day average in 2026 through July, up 14.4% YoY.

**Why it matters.** Consequences: (1) minute bars are comfortably a local-disk problem — 3,000 tickers × 5 years fits on any modern laptop, so there is no reason to compromise on RAW storage. (2) Tick data is NOT — archiving full SIP trades is ~340 GB/year compressed for the whole tape, so ticks live in a fixed-size, memory-mapped, per-symbol RING BUFFER that is recycled at session end, with only the derived bars persisted. (3) Partition Parquet by (symbol_figi, year, month), not by date alone: a chart request is one symbol × a date range, which is exactly the wrong shape for date-only partitioning. Target file size 64–256 MB per part with row groups around 100k–1m rows.

### 16. Macro data without vintages is a hindsight machine — FRED/ALFRED bitemporality is a first-class schema requirement, not a nice-to-have  
`confidence: high`

FRED's API supports realtime_start / realtime_end parameters and a fred/series/vintagedates endpoint; vintage dates are the release dates on which a series' values actually changed. ALFRED (Archival FRED) is the point-in-time layer: with realtime_start/realtime_end you can reconstruct exactly what a series looked like on any past date. Client libraries now always return realtime_start and realtime_end columns alongside observations. Rate limit: 120 requests/minute with an API key.

**Why it matters.** US GDP, payrolls, CPI and most macro series are revised, sometimes heavily. If the store keys observations on (series_id, obs_date) alone, then every historical chart, every backtest and — worst — every AI-authored knowledge pack asserting 'Q1 growth was X' will be stating a number nobody could have known at the time. Schema: PRIMARY KEY (series_id, obs_date, vintage_date), with a materialised 'latest' view for convenience. This one decision separates a research instrument from a plausible-sounding fiction generator, and it costs almost nothing to get right at the start and is agonising to retrofit.

### 17. The AI knowledge layer's economics: Claude for authoring, Voyage for embeddings, Batch API at 50% for the nightly run  
`confidence: high`

Anthropic model pricing as of 2026-06-24 (per million tokens, input/output): Claude Opus 5 (claude-opus-5) $5/$25, 1M context; Claude Sonnet 5 $2/$10; Claude Haiku 4.5 $1/$5, 200K context; Claude Fable 5 $10/$50. Message Batches run asynchronously at 50% cost; batch results return in ANY order and must be keyed by custom_id, never by position. Prompt caching is prefix-match with render order tools → system → messages; minimum cacheable prefix ~1024 tokens; max 4 breakpoints; verify with usage.cache_read_input_tokens. Anthropic ships NO embedding model — Voyage AI is the standard pairing: voyage-3.5 from $0.06 per million input tokens, Voyage AI pricing spanning free to $0.18/M as of August 2026, with the first 200 million tokens free on voyage-4-large, voyage-4, voyage-4-lite, voyage-context-4 and voyage-code-3. voyage-context-3/4 produce contextualised chunk embeddings (each chunk embedded aware of surrounding document context) — materially better for filings, where a paragraph out of context is ambiguous. Local vector store options: sqlite-vec (pure C, no dependencies, float/int8/binary vectors, virtual tables with pre-filtering, but BRUTE-FORCE only — DiskANN/IVF/HNSW are planned, not implemented); DuckDB VSS (HNSW on ARRAY columns, explicitly experimental); LanceDB (IVF-PQ, fastest but approximate with lower default recall).

**Why it matters.** Concrete plan: nightly knowledge-pack authoring goes through the Batch API (50% off, latency irrelevant overnight); Haiku 4.5 handles bulk extraction/classification of filings; Opus 5 handles synthesis and the opinionated house view. Prompt-cache the stable system prompt and house style — but only if the prefix is byte-stable, so no timestamps or per-request IDs before the last breakpoint. For the vector store: a personal corpus of ~200k chunks at 1024 dims float32 is ~800 MB and sqlite-vec's brute force is borderline (~100–300 ms); use int8 quantisation or Matryoshka dimension truncation to 512 and it comfortably fits under 100 ms with no ANN index and no index-corruption failure mode. Rebuild the embedding index into vectors/next and atomically swap, so a crashed overnight rebuild never leaves a half index.

### 18. Threat model, narrowed: infostealers harvesting API keys, and package/pack supply chain — DPAPI alone does not save you  
`confidence: high`

SpyCloud's 2026 reporting documents 18.1 million exposed API keys and tokens from malware sources alone; infostealers stole 1.8 billion credentials in H1 2025, an 800% increase over the prior six months. Most active families as of early 2026: LummaC2, ACRStealer, StealC, Vidar; Microsoft published ACR Stealer intrusion-chain analysis on 2026-07-16 and StealC/Amadey analysis on 2026-06-24. Crucially, DPAPI is not a control against this class: malware running as the logged-in user simply calls CryptUnprotectData and Windows decrypts for it. Chrome's App-Bound Encryption (Chrome 127+) has been bypassed by multiple stealer families (Stealc, Vidar, LummaC2, Meduza) and by tooling such as DumpBrowserSecrets via headless-Chromium DLL injection. Supply chain: Sonatype identified over 454,600 new malicious packages during 2025, cumulative blocked malware exceeding 1.233 million across npm, PyPI, Maven Central, NuGet and Hugging Face. NuGet-specific 2025 incidents: 'Sharp7Extend' and related packages by alias shanhai666, published 2023–2024, 9,488 downloads, carrying payloads timed to detonate in 2027–2028 against Siemens S7 PLCs, reported to NuGet 2025-11-05; and a homoglyph typosquat 'Netherеum.All' substituting a Cyrillic character for a Latin one, live 2025-10-16 to 2025-10-20. Signing options for the knowledge-pack channel: minisign (Ed25519, minimal, offline-verifiable), or Sigstore/cosign whose root of trust is distributed via TUF and which supports offline verification against a persisted Rekor bundle plus importing existing RSA/ECDSA/Ed25519 keys.

**Why it matters.** Practical, non-theatrical controls for this single-user app. (1) Assume key exfiltration is possible and make it cheap to survive: every provider key is scoped read-only by construction (the app never touches a brokerage), keys are rotated on a schedule, and the app surfaces a 'rotate all keys' action plus per-key last-used telemetry so anomalous use is visible. Store keys under DPAPI CurrentUser WITH additional entropy derived from a user passphrase held only in memory — that converts a passive file-grab into an interactive-attack requirement. (2) NuGet hygiene as CI gates, not aspiration: committed packages.lock.json with RestoreLockedMode, NuGetAudit failing the build on any advisory, package source mapping pinning every namespace to nuget.org, and a manual review gate on any new transitive dependency. Prefer few, large, well-known dependencies over many small ones. (3) Knowledge packs must be DATA-ONLY by invariant — no code, no templates that get evaluated, no SQL — Ed25519-signed with the public key pinned in the binary, signature verified BEFORE the parser touches a byte, then strict-schema validated. (4) Pack and filing text entering Claude prompts is untrusted content: delimit it, never promote it to system instructions, and give the AI layer no network egress beyond the Anthropic API and no write tools.

### 19. Scheduling on a laptop that sleeps: Quartz.NET has misfire semantics, Hangfire does not — but a watermark model beats both  
`confidence: high`

Comparative analysis (2026 sources): Quartz.NET offers rich cron triggers, calendars, misfire policies and DB-backed clustering, letting you declare per-trigger whether missed runs catch up or are skipped; Hangfire is a general enqueue/retry/dashboard background-job system with 'no real answer to misfire handling'. Both remain actively maintained on current .NET. Newer entrants (TickerQ, Coravel) exist but add nothing here.

**Why it matters.** For a machine that sleeps 22:00–08:00, misfire handling IS the requirement — a 02:00 EOD consolidation job must decide, on wake, whether to run once or not at all. Recommended shape: drive every overnight job off a WATERMARK, not a schedule. Each job stores last_success_watermark; on trigger it computes work as 'everything between watermark and now' and is fully idempotent, so whether it fires on time, late, or twice makes no difference to the result. Quartz.NET then supplies only the trigger, with FireAndProceed for consolidation-type jobs and DoNothing for pollers that will recur anyway. Also subscribe to SystemEvents.PowerModeChanged (Resume) and SystemEvents.TimeChanged to force an immediate reconciliation sweep on wake or clock change — the wall clock may have moved hours while you were suspended, and every staleness badge and every scheduled deadline is now wrong until you recompute.

### 20. The academic case for the insiders pillar is specific enough to be an ingestion requirement, not just a marketing line  
`confidence: high`

Cohen, Malloy and Pomorski (Journal of Finance, 2012, 'Decoding Inside Information') separate ROUTINE from OPPORTUNISTIC insider trades: a portfolio built solely on opportunistic trades yields value-weighted abnormal returns of 82 basis points per month, while abnormal returns to routine traders are essentially zero. Routine trades comprise over half the entire universe of insider trades, and stripping them leaves the subset containing all the predictive power. Separately, cluster trading — several insiders buying within a short window, typically defined as 3+ insiders including independent directors within 30 days — historically produces roughly double the excess return of single buys; more recent work finds clusters not exclusively formed of routine trades outperform non-cluster opportunistic purchases, which in turn outperform routine trades. Seyhun and Lakonishok–Lee independently find insider purchases predict positive abnormal returns.

**Why it matters.** This dictates the schema, not just the UI. To classify a trade as routine vs opportunistic you need each insider's MULTI-YEAR trade history across ALL issuers — the classic operationalisation is 'has this person traded in the same calendar month for several consecutive years'. That is only possible if you have (a) the person-level CIK identity graph, (b) full Form 4 history, not just recent filings, and (c) it computed per-person-globally, not per-issuer. Similarly, cluster detection needs a rolling 30-day window join across reporting owners of the same issuer with the director/officer flags attached. Both must be materialised as derived tables in the overnight job, because computing them on demand across a decade of filings will not render in a panel.

### 21. Non-US insider/ownership data is fragmented with no central machine-readable source — plan for asymmetric global coverage and say so in the UI  
`confidence: medium`

EU/UK MAR Article 19: PDMRs and closely associated persons must notify the issuer and the national competent authority, and the issuer must make it public within three working days. Default notification threshold is EUR 20,000 aggregate per calendar year, which member states may raise to EUR 50,000 or lower to EUR 10,000 at their discretion. There is no centralised ESMA machine-readable PDMR database — each NCA (e.g. the Dutch AFM) maintains its own register, in its own format, and UK disclosure flows through an RIS to the FCA's National Storage Mechanism. Japan: EDINET is run by the FSA with an official API (v2, free API key required) serving large-shareholding reports (大量保有報告書) in PDF, XBRL and CSV. Canada: SEDI (sedi.ca) is a browser-based system for insider reports with no clean bulk API; third parties (INK Research/Canadian Insider) sell extracts, and community tooling exists to scrape the insider-transaction-detail pages to CSV.

**Why it matters.** The honest engineering answer is that the insiders pillar is a US-first product, with Japan as the best second (real API, XBRL) and Europe/Canada as scraper-grade with per-jurisdiction adapters and per-country threshold rules. Do not promise uniform global coverage in the data model; instead make coverage an explicit, queryable attribute — every issuer carries an insider_coverage_level (STRUCTURED / SEMI / NONE) that the UI renders, so the absence of insider activity on a European name is visibly 'we don't see it here' rather than falsely 'nothing happened'. That distinction is exactly the kind of thing a naive plan gets wrong and a sophisticated user notices immediately.

### 22. Vendors die and endpoints vanish without warning — IEX Cloud is the cautionary case and the reason provider abstraction is not over-engineering  
`confidence: high`

IEX Cloud retired its API products on 2024-08-31, giving customers approximately three months' notice, after IEX decided to refocus on core exchange operations. Thousands of dashboards, bots and trackers broke. The migration cohort scattered across Financial Modeling Prep, Alpha Vantage, Polygon/Massive, Intrinio, Finage and Tiingo. In the same period, Polygon.io itself rebranded to Massive with a domain change (polygon.io now 301s to massive.com), and Databento discontinued usage-based pricing for LIVE equities data in favour of subscription plans. EDGAR's own full-text search endpoint carries no deprecation policy or schema commitment whatsoever.

**Why it matters.** This is the empirical justification for the capability-interface design: IQuoteProvider, IBarProvider, IFundamentalsProvider, IFilingsProvider, INewsProvider, IMacroProvider, IFxProvider, IReferenceProvider, each with a declared capability + entitlement descriptor, and a router over them. The concrete test that proves the abstraction works is a 'vendor eviction drill': a scripted exercise that disables the primary equities provider entirely and asserts the app still renders bars, quotes (degraded/delayed), and a correct staleness badge from the secondary. Run it as part of CI against recorded fixtures. A provider abstraction nobody has ever swapped is decoration.
