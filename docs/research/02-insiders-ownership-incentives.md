### 3. Form 4 XML schema: the 10b5-1 flag is DOCUMENT-level (<aff10b5One>), and prices can be footnotes rather than numbers  
`confidence: high`

Verified by fetching a live filing (Apple Inc., accession 0001140361-26-025622, form4.xml, 2026-06-17). Root <ownershipDocument>, <schemaVersion>X0609</schemaVersion> (upgraded from X0508; the 10b5-1 checkbox arrived in EDGAR Release 23.1 on 2023-03-20, mandatory for all Form 4/5 from that date). Structure: <issuer>{issuerCik, issuerName, issuerTradingSymbol}, <reportingOwner>{rptOwnerCik, rptOwnerName, reportingOwnerRelationship{isDirector,isOfficer,isTenPercentOwner,isOther,officerTitle}}, then <aff10b5One>false</aff10b5One> as a SIBLING of the tables — i.e. it flags the whole filing, not the individual transaction. Then <nonDerivativeTable>/<nonDerivativeTransaction> and <derivativeTable>/<derivativeTransaction>, each with transactionCoding{transactionFormType, transactionCode, equitySwapInvolved}, transactionAmounts{transactionShares, transactionPricePerShare, transactionAcquiredDisposedCode}, postTransactionAmounts{sharesOwnedFollowingTransaction}, ownershipNature{directOrIndirectOwnership, natureOfOwnership}. CRITICAL TRAP observed in that very filing: <transactionPricePerShare><footnoteId id="F1"/></transactionPricePerShare> — no <value> element at all. Weighted-average-price sales routinely appear as a footnote with a price RANGE, not a number. The 10b5-1 plan ADOPTION DATE is not a field; it lives in free-text <footnote>.

**Why it matters.** Every naive Form 4 parser breaks on the footnote-instead-of-price case and silently drops or zero-fills the trade. The doc-level 10b5-1 flag means a mixed Form 4 (plan sale + tax withholding) cannot be disambiguated from the boolean alone. Extracting plan adoption dates — the single most valuable 10b5-1 signal, because it lets you measure the cooling-off gap and detect plan adoption immediately before good news — requires NLP over footnotes. That is an AI-knowledge-layer job, and it is a genuine differentiator.

### 5. Form 4/5 transaction code taxonomy — the difference between signal and noise  
`confidence: high`

General: P = open-market or private purchase; S = open-market or private sale; V = transaction voluntarily reported earlier than required. Rule 16b-3 exempt: A = grant/award/other acquisition from the issuer; D = disposition to the issuer (e.g. share buyback participation, RSU settlement to company); F = payment of exercise price or tax liability by delivering/withholding securities ('sell-to-cover'); I = discretionary transaction under a 16b-3(f) plan (401(k)-type); M = exercise or conversion of a derivative security exempt under 16b-3. Derivative: C = conversion of derivative; E = expiration of short derivative position; H = expiration/cancellation of long derivative position with value received; O = exercise of out-of-the-money derivative; X = exercise of in-the-money or at-the-money derivative. Other 16(b)-exempt/small acquisitions: G = bona fide gift; L = small acquisition under Rule 16a-6; W = acquisition/disposition by will or laws of descent; Z = deposit into or withdrawal from a voting trust. Other: J = other acquisition/disposition (describe); K = equity swap or similar instrument; U = disposition pursuant to a tender of shares in a change-of-control. A code suffixed with '4' (e.g. 'P4') means the transaction should have been reported earlier on a Form 4 — i.e. it is late.

**Why it matters.** Only P is an unambiguous conviction signal. The M->S and M->F pairs are compensation mechanics dressed as selling and are the single largest source of false 'insider dumping' headlines. G (gift) is the known laundering route — a gift to a family trust/charity that then sells is economically a sale with none of the Form 4 optics; academic work has documented backdated gift timing. K (equity swap) and J are where economic exposure gets hidden. The '4' suffix and Form 5 codes let you build a delinquency/late-filing flag, which is itself predictive and now enforcement-relevant.

### 7. Form 144 has been mandatory-electronic and structured since 13 April 2023 — it is a 1–3 day LEAD indicator on Form 4 sales  
`confidence: high`

SEC mandated electronic Form 144 filing on EDGAR in structured XML from 13 April 2023 (paper/PDF eliminated). Form 144 is a NOTICE OF PROPOSED SALE by an affiliate under Rule 144 — filed concurrently with placing the sell order, i.e. BEFORE the sale settles and before the Form 4 (which is due 2 business days after execution). It carries: security, broker, approximate date of sale, number of shares, aggregate market value, exchange, and the acquisition history table. Form 144 also gets the 22:00 ET extended EDGAR window. Rule 144 volume limits for affiliates: greater of 1% of outstanding shares or the average weekly reported volume over the preceding 4 calendar weeks, per rolling 3-month period.

**Why it matters.** Almost no retail insider product surfaces Form 144. It is a free, structured, forward-looking sell signal that front-runs the Form 4 by days, and reconciling 144-filed-but-never-sold against Form 4s tells you which insiders flinched. Also: 144 filings that repeatedly max the 1%/ADV cap identify a systematic liquidator vs a one-off.

### 8. Schedule 13D/13G: deadlines were compressed in 2024 AND the filings became machine-readable XML on 18 December 2024  
`confidence: high`

Under the SEC's Modernization of Beneficial Ownership Reporting (adopted Oct 2023): initial Schedule 13D now due within 5 BUSINESS days of crossing 5% (was 10 calendar days); 13D amendments due within 2 business days of the material change. Schedule 13G deadlines took effect 30 September 2024: Qualified Institutional Investors file within 45 days after the quarter-end in which they exceed 5% (or 5 business days after month-end if they exceed 10%); Passive Investors (<20%, no control intent) within 5 business days; ALL 13G amendments now due within 45 days after the quarter-end in which any material change occurred (previously annual, within 45 days of calendar year-end). Separately, from 18 December 2024 all Schedules 13D and 13G must be filed in structured, machine-readable XML (exhibits remain HTML) — quantitative disclosures, narrative text and identification checkboxes are all tagged.

**Why it matters.** Two things. (1) Activist stakes now surface ~5 days faster and 13G stale-data windows shrank from up to 12 months to ~45 days after quarter-end — ownership snapshots are far fresher than most products assume. (2) The XML mandate means 13D Item 4 'Purpose of Transaction' language is now parseable at scale — you can classify activist intent (board seats, strategic alternatives, sale of company) programmatically instead of by hand. Pre-2024 filings are unstructured text; any historical backfill needs two parsers.

### 13. Buybacks: the 2023 daily-disclosure rule was VACATED — Item 703 monthly-in-quarterly is still the law  
`confidence: high`

The SEC adopted the Share Repurchase Disclosure Modernization rule in May 2023 (daily repurchase data tagged in Inline XBRL, filed quarterly as Exhibit 26, plus narrative rationale and a 10b5-1/insider-trading-proximity checkbox). On 31 October 2023 the Fifth Circuit held the SEC acted arbitrarily and capriciously (APA) and gave 30 days to cure; the SEC could not, and on 19 December 2023 the court VACATED the rule. The SEC issued technical amendments (Release 34-99778, Federal Register 8 April 2024) removing the vacated text. As of 2026 the operative requirement reverts to Item 703 of Regulation S-K: tabular disclosure in each 10-Q/10-K of, BY MONTH for the quarter, total shares purchased, average price paid per share, shares purchased under publicly announced programs, and maximum shares/dollar value that may yet be purchased. Rule 10b-18 remains a non-exclusive SAFE HARBOUR with four conditions on any single day: (a) manner — one broker/dealer for all Rule 10b-18 purchases that day; (b) timing — no purchases at the open, and no purchases in the last 10 minutes (last 30 minutes for securities below the ADTV/public-float thresholds); (c) price — not above the higher of the last independent transaction price or the highest independent published bid; (d) volume — <=25% of the security's 4-week ADTV, with a once-weekly block-purchase alternative if no other 10b-18 purchases occur that day.

**Why it matters.** Any product promising 'daily buyback execution data' for US issuers is fabricating. What you CAN do — and few do — is derive implied buyback execution from the Item 703 monthly table against the month's VWAP to score whether management bought well or badly, and cross it against Form 4 selling in the same month. Company buying while the CEO sells is the highest-value divergence in the entire dataset and it is computable from two free sources.

### 14. Rule 10b5-1 post-2022 architecture: cooling-off periods, certifications, and the quarterly Item 408(a) table  
`confidence: high`

Amendments adopted 14 December 2022 (Release 33-11138). Conditions apply to plans adopted or modified on or after 27 February 2023. Cooling-off for DIRECTORS AND OFFICERS: trading may not begin until the LATER of (i) 90 days after adoption/modification, or (ii) two business days after disclosure of the issuer's financial results for the fiscal quarter of adoption — subject to a maximum of 120 days. For persons other than the issuer and directors/officers: 30 days. Issuers themselves: no cooling-off period. ### 16. The academic evidence on what actually works in insider signals — with numbers  
`confidence: high`

Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski, 'Decoding Inside Information', Journal of Finance 2012 (NBER w16454, 2010): classifying insiders as ROUTINE (same calendar month in each of the prior 3 years) vs OPPORTUNISTIC; routine trades are >50% of the universe and carry essentially zero abnormal return, while a portfolio of opportunistic trades earns 82 basis points per month value-weighted abnormal return (~10%/yr), and opportunistic trades predict future firm news and events. Jeng, Metrick & Zeckhauser, Review of Economics and Statistics 85(2) 2003, 453–471 (1975–1996): insider PURCHASES earn abnormal returns >6%/yr — 52 to 68 bp/month over the first six months, with ~25% of the abnormal return accruing within 5 days and ~50% within one month; insider SALES earn no significant abnormal return. Lakonishok & Lee, 'Are Insiders' Trades Informative?' (NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq 1975–1995): purchases outperform sales, effect concentrated in SMALL-CAP and small-growth names. Seyhun (1986, 60,000 transactions, 769 NYSE/AMEX firms 1975–1981): chairmen and officer-directors are materially better predictors than other insiders; Seyhun (1992): aggregate net insider purchases explained up to 60% of variation in one-year-ahead aggregate stock returns (1975–1989). Cluster effects: Alldredge & Blank (2017, US 1986–2014) find cluster purchases within two days earn 2.1% monthly abnormal return vs 1.2% for solitary purchases; Kang, Kim & Wang (2018, 1986–2016) find 3.8% vs 2.0% over 21 trading days, widening by ~2.5pp over 90 days, with top-executive clusters most informative. Europe: Dardas (2011, 17 Western European countries, Jan 2002–Dec 2009) — high-conviction purchases 20.94% average 12-month excess return, medium 1.32%, low -3.40%.

**Why it matters.** This is the specification for the scoring engine, not decoration. It dictates: weight P over everything; weight CEO/CFO/Chairman over directors and over 10% holders; weight clusters (>=2 insiders within 2 trading days) heavily; strip routine calendar traders; scale by trade size relative to the insider's existing holding and to their salary; expect the signal to decay fast (half the return in the first month) — which means the terminal must be a real-time alerting product, not a weekly digest. And it says sales are mostly noise, which is the opposite of what retail insider products emphasise.

### 17. Form 13F: what it hides, when it arrives, and the confidential-treatment escape hatch (now much harder to use)  
`confidence: high`

### 29. Governance red flags that are structured, free, and almost never surfaced together  
`confidence: high`

8-K item codes come free in the submissions API `items` field, so a red-flag monitor is nearly zero-cost: Item 1.01 material definitive agreement; Item 2.02 results of operations (earnings); Item 3.01 notice of delisting/failure to satisfy listing rule; Item 4.01 changes in registrant's certifying accountant (with the required former-auditor letter under Item 304 of Reg S-K disclosing any disagreements or reportable events); Item 4.02 non-reliance on previously issued financial statements (the restatement bomb); Item 5.02 departure/election/appointment of directors and principal officers; Item 5.07 submission of matters to a vote of security holders — the say-on-pay result, due within 4 business days of the meeting, with preliminary results permitted and an 8-K/A within 4 business days of finals; Item 7.01/8.01 Reg FD and other events. Item 405 of Reg S-K requires the issuer to name insiders who filed Section 16 reports late. Item 404 requires related-person transaction disclosure (generally transactions above USD 120,000 involving directors, officers, 5% holders or their immediate family). Item 403 beneficial ownership tables carry the PLEDGED-SHARES footnotes — margin-loan pledges by a founder/CEO are a recognised forced-selling and control risk (they were central to multiple 2015–2021 blowups) and Exhibit 19 insider-trading policies now let you check whether the company even permits pledging. NT 10-K / NT 10-Q (Rule 12b-25): must be filed no later than one business day after the original due date; grants 15 additional calendar days for an annual report and 5 for a quarterly, and requires a statement of the reason — a late 10-K plus an auditor change plus a CFO departure inside 90 days is the classic pre-blowup triple.

**Why it matters.** Individually these are commodity facts. Composited into a single time-ordered 'governance stress' timeline per issuer — auditor change, NT filing, Item 4.02, CFO exit, spike in Form 4 selling, rising pledged shares, failed say-on-pay — they form a genuinely institutional-grade product surface that no retail terminal ships, built entirely from free structured fields.

### 30. Operational constraints and gotchas that will bite the build  
`confidence: high`

(1) sec.gov and data.sec.gov do NOT support CORS — an Electron/WebView2 renderer cannot fetch them directly with web security enabled; all SEC traffic must go through the app's own process. (2) The 10 req/s limit is per source IP 'regardless of the number of machines' — a global token bucket is mandatory, not per-worker. Exceeding it yields an HTML block page with a Reference ID, not a JSON error, so the HTTP client must detect content-type mismatches. (3) Outbound FTP is commonly blocked in restricted network environments (verified here: ftp://ftp3.interactivebrokers.com timed out while HTTPS worked) — any design relying on vendor FTP drops needs an HTTPS alternative. (4) EDGAR Next went live 24 March 2025 with a filer transition deadline of 12 September 2025 (enrolment window to 19 December 2025); it changes filer authentication and adds filer-side APIs but does NOT change the public data APIs — however the SEC has stated substantial API-ecosystem changes will be previewed in the EDGAR Beta environment, so treat public endpoints as versioned, not eternal. (5) File-naming conventions change without notice: 13F data sets moved from {YYYY}q{Q}_form13f.zip to date-range names like 01mar2026-31may2026_form13f.zip in March 2024. (6) Form 4 amendments (4/A) restate prior filings — a naive append-only store will double-count; key on (issuerCik, rptOwnerCik, transactionDate, code, shares) and supersede by accession. (7) Insider CIKs are stable and the submissions API works for REPORTING-OWNER CIKs too, which is how you follow one person across every company they touch — the single most useful join in the dataset and one almost no product exposes.

**Why it matters.** These are the failure modes that turn a working prototype into a silently-wrong product. Items (6) and (7) in particular determine the data model: entity-centric (person and institution as first-class entities) rather than filing-centric.

## Recommended decisions

### Build the incentives pillar directly on SEC/regulator primary sources, or buy an aggregator API?

**Recommendation.** Build on primary sources for 100% of the regulatory substance. Buy exactly three things: sec-api.io Business at USD 239/mo (or USD 199/mo annual) ONLY if sub-second real-time push proves necessary; Ortex Advanced at USD 129/mo for borrow rate and utilisation; and Quiver API Hobbyist at USD 300/yr for pre-OCR'd congressional data. Start with the free path for all three and upgrade on demonstrated need.

**Rationale.** Every fact the pillar depends on — Form 3/4/5, 144, 13D/G, 13F, DEF 14A, 8-K, Item 703, short interest, FTDs, threshold lists, COT — is free, keyless and (verified live this session) reliably accessible at 10 req/sec. Aggregators add latency, an extra failure mode, licence restrictions (Quiver and Unusual Whales self-serve plans carry no commercial-use rights) and, critically, THEIR interpretation of transaction codes and footnotes, which is exactly the layer where the product's differentiation lives. The only genuine gaps are securities-lending economics — which will have no free public source until dissemination begins 29 March 2029 at the earliest — and OCR of handwritten House PTRs.

**Rejected.** A full institutional stack (2iQ / VerityData InsiderScore / Washington Service / S3 BLACK) is quote-only, per-seat annual licensing plainly aimed at funds — almost certainly five figures per year for a single user, and it would hand the product's core interpretation layer to a vendor. Retail aggregators (Fintel USD 10.95–95/mo, WhaleWisdom USD 300–500/yr) are cheap but shallow, US-only, and offer nothing the primary sources do not.

**Cost.** Free core. Optional: Ortex USD 468–1,548/yr; Quiver USD 300/yr; sec-api.io USD 2,388–2,868/yr if real-time streaming is added. Realistic year-one total USD 800–4,700.

### Polling architecture for real-time filings — SEC Atom/index polling versus a paid WebSocket stream?

**Recommendation.** Poll. Run a single global 10 req/sec token bucket in the app's main process (never the renderer — data.sec.gov has no CORS). Poll browse-edgar?action=getcurrent every 20–30 seconds with an aggressive 16:00–22:00 ET cadence for Section 16 forms, reconcile against the daily form.{YYYYMMDD}.idx at end of day, and backfill from the quarterly Form 345 zips. Treat any HTML response as a rate-limit block, not an error.

**Rationale.** The SEC states submissions-API latency under one second from dissemination, and there is no embargo or tiered release — polling at 20–30s costs you at most tens of seconds against a paid stream, which is immaterial for a discretionary analyst but is the difference between USD 0 and USD 2,868/yr. The genuinely time-critical window is 16:00–22:00 ET, which is a scheduling problem, not a technology problem.

**Rejected.** sec-api.io's WebSocket Filing Stream is genuinely good and the fallback if polling proves unreliable at scale; rejected for v1 because the latency advantage does not change any decision this user makes. Third-party RSS mirrors were rejected as an uncontrolled dependency.

**Cost.** USD 0 vs USD 2,388–2,868/yr for sec-api.io Business.

### How to handle the short-side data gap given Form SHO and Rule 10c-1a are dead until 2028/2029?

**Recommendation.** Build the free stack now — FINRA bi-monthly short interest with the verified 2026 calendar, FINRA daily off-exchange short-volume files, SEC cnsfails fails-to-deliver, SRO Reg SHO threshold lists with a 13-settlement-day close-out countdown — and license Ortex for borrow rate, utilisation and estimated real-time short interest. Design the short-interest ingest so reporting frequency, turnaround and field set are configuration, not code, because SR-FINRA-2026-012 may convert this to weekly/T+1 with new synthetic-short and FTD-allocation fields.

**Rationale.** Form SHO is exempted until 2 January 2028 and 10c-1a dissemination until 29 March 2029; there is no path to free granular borrow data in the product's foreseeable life. Meanwhile a rule change that materially improves the free data is sitting at the SEC right now with an August 2026 decision date. Building for configurability costs little and hedges both ways.

**Rejected.** S3 Partners BLACK is the institutional standard but is quote-only and distributed through Bloomberg/Eikon/Eze — wrong channel and wrong price point. Waiting for Form SHO is not an option on a 2026 build. Using FINRA short VOLUME as a proxy for short INTEREST is rejected outright: it is off-exchange only and includes market-maker hedging, so it is systematically wrong in exactly the crowded names the user cares about.

**Cost.** Ortex Basic USD 39/mo or Advanced USD 129/mo (USD 468–1,548/yr); everything else free.

### US-only at launch, or global insider coverage from day one?

**Recommendation.** Ship the US at full depth in v1, but architect the schema as jurisdiction-agnostic from the first commit (jurisdiction, regime, threshold_currency, threshold_amount, threshold_effective_from, deadline_business_days, source_document_url on every insider record), and land UK NSM/RNS and Japan EDINET in v1.1 — they are the two highest value-per-unit-effort non-US regimes. Canada SEDI, ASX 3Y, HKEX DI and SEBI 7(2) follow in v1.2.

**Rationale.** US-only is a solved, commoditised problem — OpenInsider does it free. Global coverage is the moat and it is what an institutional-calibre discretionary user actually needs. But retrofitting jurisdiction and threshold metadata onto a US-shaped schema is expensive and error-prone, and the EU threshold jump from EUR 5,000 to 20,000 to 50,000 (DE/FR/IT/DK from 1 Jan 2026) proves the metadata is load-bearing, not decorative. UK and Japan first because UK NSM is free, complete and English, and Japan is both under-covered and undergoing a live regime change on 1 May 2026 (33% → 30% TOB trigger, joint-holder reform).

**Rejected.** Licensing 2iQ for instant 50-country coverage — attractive but quote-only institutional pricing, and it would outsource the interpretive layer that is the product's reason to exist. Global-first at launch — rejected as it delays a shippable product by months.

**Cost.** Engineering time only; all sources free. UK ~2–3 weeks (RNS template parsing), Japan ~3–4 weeks (EDINET key + XBRL + Japanese-language handling), each of Canada/Australia/HK/India ~2–4 weeks.

### Where does Claude's authored knowledge layer add value that structured parsing cannot?

**Recommendation.** Point Claude at exactly the places where the schema runs out: (1) Form 4/144 footnotes — weighted-average price ranges, 10b5-1 adoption dates, indirect-ownership chains; (2) Schedule 13D Item 4 activist-intent classification; (3) CD&A and Item 402(v)/(x) narrative to extract which metrics management is paid on and translate that into named accounting risks; (4) Exhibit 19 insider-trading policies for pledging/hedging/pre-clearance terms; (5) Item 404 related-party narratives; (6) a scheduled per-company Incentives Dossier that refreshes on filing events; and (7) the Regulatory Change Watchdog register. Every generated assertion must carry an accession number and filing date.

**Rationale.** Structured XBRL and XML give you the numbers; the interpretation lives in prose that no vendor has indexed at scale. This is also where the terminal earns the 'Rothschild-school analyst' framing — the questions are qualitative (who really controls this, what is management incentivised to do, what does the pledge structure imply under stress) and the evidence is textual. Enforcing accession-level citation makes the AI layer auditable, which is the only way a sophisticated user will trust it.

**Rejected.** Using Claude for numeric extraction that XBRL/XML already provides — wasteful and less reliable than a parser. Free-form unfootnoted commentary — rejected as unauditable and therefore worthless to this user.

**Cost.** LLM inference on a scheduled cadence; scoped to a watchlist of tens-to-hundreds of names rather than the full universe, this is a modest recurring cost.

### Data model: filing-centric or entity-centric?

**Recommendation.** Entity-centric. Make PERSON (reporting-owner CIK), INSTITUTION (13F filer CIK / CIK family), and ISSUER first-class entities; model filings as immutable events attached to them, keyed on (issuerCik, ownerCik, transactionDate, transactionCode, shares, accession) with 4/A amendments superseding rather than appending.

**Rationale.** Two hard facts force this. First, data.sec.gov/submissions works for reporting-owner CIKs, so following one person across every issuer they touch is a single free API call — the most valuable join in the dataset and one virtually no product exposes. Second, Form 4/A amendments restate prior filings, so an append-only filing-centric store silently double-counts. Entity-centric also makes board interlocks, Insider Passport, hit-rate history and the routine/opportunistic classifier natural rather than bolted-on.

**Rejected.** Filing-centric (the natural shape of EDGAR) — simpler to ingest, but it makes the product's best features either impossible or wrong.

**Cost.** Design decision; no incremental cost if made at the start, expensive to retrofit.

## Risks

- Fabrication risk on Form SHO. Rule 13f-2 short-position reporting does not produce data until ~February 2028 and Rule 10c-1a securities-lending dissemination until 29 March 2029. Any design document or LLM-generated plan asserting manager-level short position data is available in 2026 is wrong. Similarly, the vacated 2023 daily buyback rule means daily US repurchase data does not exist.
- SR-FINRA-2026-012 is unresolved. The SEC's designated decision date of 14 August 2026 has passed with no order located as of 2026-08-24. Short interest could move to weekly/T+1 with new synthetic-short and fail-to-deliver-allocation fields at short notice, or the proposal could go to proceedings and stall for a year. Hardcoding bimonthly cadence or the current field set is a near-term breakage.
- SEC IP blocking. The 10 req/sec fair-access limit is per source IP 'regardless of the number of machines', enforcement is real (verified: a generic Mozilla User-Agent triggered an immediate block page), and the block returns HTML rather than a JSON error. A single misconfigured backfill can take the entire product offline for the user, with no vendor to call.
- Handwritten congressional PTRs. A material share of House Periodic Transaction Reports are scanned handwritten PDFs. OCR error rates on ticker symbols and amount bands are non-trivial and errors are silent. Senate eFD has no bulk archive and requires session/CSRF handling that breaks on site changes.
- Congressional data may be legislated out of existence. The House passed a trading ban 232–198 in July 2026 and Senate bills (PELOSI Act, HONEST Act) are advancing. If enacted, the congressional-trading feature loses its data source entirely — it must not be a load-bearing pillar.
- EU threshold discontinuity corrupts time series. The Article 19 threshold went EUR 5,000 → 20,000 (Listing Act) → 50,000 in Germany, France, Italy and Denmark from 1 January 2026, while the UK stayed at EUR 5,000 and Malta went to EUR 10,000. Any un-normalised European insider time series will show a spurious collapse in activity across 2025–2026.
- Silent data loss from retention limits. BaFin retains directors'-dealings records for only 12 months from publication; HKEX CCASS exposes a rolling 12 months; Capitol Trades limits on-site history to 3 years. History that is not harvested continuously is permanently gone.
- Form 4 price fields are frequently absent. Weighted-average-price transactions carry only a <footnoteId> where <value> would be, with the price RANGE in prose. Verified in a live Apple Form 4 this session. A parser that zero-fills or drops these will systematically understate executive sale values.
- 10b5-1 flag granularity mismatch. <aff10b5One> is document-level, so a Form 4 mixing a plan sale with a tax-withholding disposition cannot be disambiguated from the boolean. Reporting 'this was a 10b5-1 sale' from the flag alone will sometimes be wrong.
- Fragile external dependencies with known dates. lda.senate.gov retires after 30 June 2026 (migrate to LDA.gov with an API key). EDGAR Beta previews 'substantial updates to the SEC's API ecosystem'. The 13F data-set file-naming convention already changed once (2024). Hardcoded hosts and filename patterns will break.
- N-PORT regime uncertainty. The 2024 monthly-public amendments were suspended and a 2026 proposal would scale them back to 45-day filing and quarterly public disclosure, with compliance pushed to 17 Nov 2027 / 18 May 2028. The final rule is not settled — do not promise monthly fund holdings.
- Redistribution and licensing exposure. Quiver's self-serve API tiers and Unusual Whales' plans carry no commercial-use rights. Fine for a single-user personal terminal; fatal if the product is ever shared, sold or its outputs published.
- Legal boundary of insider signals. The product is read-only and consumes only public regulatory filings, which is lawful — but the 13F/13D/G/PDMR data it aggregates can identify positions of specific persons, and market-abuse regimes differ by jurisdiction. Keep the product informational and avoid anything that could be construed as tipping or as disseminating information ahead of a regulated publication.
- No CORS on SEC endpoints. Any Electron/WebView2 renderer attempting direct fetches to data.sec.gov or sec.gov will fail. All SEC traffic must be proxied through the app's own process — an architectural constraint that is cheap to honour up front and painful to retrofit.
- Signal decay and overfitting. Jeng/Metrick/Zeckhauser found ~25% of the insider-purchase abnormal return accrues within 5 days and ~50% within one month; the published academic edges are from samples ending 2009–2016 and may have decayed as the data became commoditised. Backtested scores must be presented with out-of-sample caveats, not as guarantees.
