CNPT

Concepts

one idea, applied to two asset classes

Argus is named for the hundred-eyed watchman. It is a surveillance instrument pointed at disclosure — the paper trail that companies, insiders, funds and large speculators are compelled to produce — normalised, scored, and put next to the price.

The organising insight

Price is the most crowded data in finance and the least differentiated. Everyone has it. What almost nobody assembles properly is the obligation trail: the filings and reports that people with better information are legally required to publish, on a schedule, whether they want to or not.

That trail is almost entirely free at origin. SEC EDGAR, N-PX voting records, FINRA short data, the FCA and ESMA short registers, CFTC Commitments of Traders, the Bank Participation Report, LBMA vault holdings, World Gold Council flows, Swiss customs, Cboe's option chain, FRED. The moat is not a data licence — it is normalisation, latency discipline, vintage honesty and signal construction. Which means it is defensible only by building it properly, and that is what most of this plan is about.

Concept 1 — Incentives as a lens, not a screen

Every other product treats insider data as a screener you visit. Argus treats it as a lens applied to everything. Open any equity and the incentive structure is already there: who bought with their own money, who sold on a schedule, which metric the CEO is paid to hit, who pledged shares to a bank, which institutions voted against the pay plan. The chart carries an incentives ribbon — insider buys, 13D crossings, plan adoptions, grant dates plotted on the price axis — so the question "what were people who knew doing here?" is answered by looking, not by navigating.

Concept 2 — The lens survives translation into FX and metals

Gold and currencies have no Form 4, and this is where a lazy plan gives up and calls FX "context". The underlying question survives intact: who holds it, what are they compelled to disclose, and what does their behaviour reveal that their commentary does not?

The two asset classes meet at the gold miners

This is the part that makes the product one thing rather than two bolted together. Producer hedge books and miner insider transactions are gold positioning data that arrives as equity disclosure. When Newmont's hedge book changes, that is a gold signal filed on EDGAR. When a mining CFO buys her own stock, that is a view on the metal. Argus reads both engines against the same forty names, and the equity watchlist carries a HEDGE column that no equity product has and no gold product can produce.

The same caveat governs both sides: most Form 4 selling is mechanical tax withholding, and most producer hedging is project-finance covenant. Separate mechanical from discretionary, score only the discretionary. One decoder, two asset classes.

Concept 3 — A knowledge base with an author

Generic RAG chatbots over your own documents are shipping everywhere and are worth little: they retrieve what you already had. Argus ships something different — a curated, versioned, signed knowledge base that I write and maintain, composed of claims with stable identity, confidence scores, decay dates and sources. Because claims persist across versions, the terminal can show you exactly what changed last night and why. See AI; the mechanism is specified in full there.

Concept 4 — Vintage honesty as a design primitive

Every number in finance has two dates: when it describes and when it arrived. A 13F is 45 days stale by law and can describe a position 135 days old. COT is Tuesday's data released Friday. Vault holdings are monthly. Form 4s are near-live. Argus never blends them into one "as of". Each row carries its own vintage badge, and the AI is told the age of every fact it reasons over. This is the single design decision that most separates a trustworthy terminal from a fast one.

The invariant everything hangs from

Argus must never become a distributor. One person seeing full-depth Nasdaq TotalView pays $15.00/month as a non-professional subscriber; an external distributor of the identical feed pays a four-figure monthly fee plus per-subscriber charges. That is not a pricing tier, it is a category change, and the trigger is a second human seeing the screen. The architecture enforces it — no network listener, no export of raw licensed ticks, no hosted view — rather than noting it in a README.

SCRN

Mockups

nine screens · live HTML, not pictures

These are rendered HTML — the type, density, colour and column structure are the specification, not an impression of it. They render dark on any page theme because the terminal commits to a single dark surface. Every figure is stamped SAMPLE DATA: tickers are real companies, but every filing, price, holder and name is illustrative. Nothing here is a record of an actual transaction.

What the screens are arguing

  • Density is the feature. 20–24px rows, hairline rules, tabular figures, no cards, no shadows. Whitespace in a blotter is paid for in scroll events.
  • Every number carries its own vintage. A 13F row is 45 days stale by law, short interest is bi-monthly, a Form 4 is near-live. Nothing is blended into a single "as of".
  • Fact and inference are different surfaces, not different tones. Facts come from deterministic pipelines with no model in the path. Any numeric AI claim without a citation is struck before render.
  • Failure is a designed screen. A dead provider greys its panel and stamps the age of the last good value. It never renders stale data as live, and never silently fails over.
  • Both asset classes get the same rigour. Screen 01 puts equities, FX and metals side by side, each carrying the lens that applies to it.

Features a plan like this normally forgets

Each of these came out of research or adversarial review, not a brainstorm, and each quietly decides whether the terminal is trustworthy:

  • The 23×5 session engine. The UTP tape moves to 23 hours a day, five days a week on 6 December 2026, Sunday 20:00 ET to Friday 20:00 ET, with new overnight LULD band indicators. Any model where "trading day" equals "calendar date" is structurally wrong within months. A session must be a named, venue-specific interval.
  • FIGI-keyed identity. Tickers are mutable display labels — reuse, spin-offs, mergers and delistings all corrupt a ticker-keyed store.
  • The delisting-to-bankruptcy path. Halt → suspension → Form 25 → Form 15 → Chapter 11. At Form 15 filings stop permanently, and a naive system renders a dead company as a coverage gap rather than a terminal state.
  • Timezone triple-book-keeping. Exchange local time, UTC, broker server time and your own are four distinct clocks. Store UTC with explicit session tags.
  • Returning after two weeks away. A "what did I miss" digest, not an unreadable backlog.
  • The Sunday FX open and the closing auction — both real states with their own liquidity behaviour.
  • Corporate-action-triggered cache invalidation. A split rewrites history; the cache must know.
  • Bulk-dataset provider abstraction. Some regulatory data ships as ZIPs, not APIs. A provider layer modelling only per-request calls designs multi-week crawls for data that downloads in minutes.

Non-goals

Deliberately excluded, each for a reason: order execution and broker connectivity (a different regulatory and security object); anything multi-user — sharing, hosted views, publishing (triggers the redistribution cliff); signals-as-a-service or copy-trading; social features; alt data with PII (credit-card panels, geolocation — MNPI and privacy hazard for no actionable edge); automated strategy execution; and third-party plugins or MCP servers, which would puncture the AI boundary that the whole security model rests on.

Why non-professional status is load-bearing

Full-depth Nasdaq TotalView is $15.00/month for a non-professional subscriber against $80.50–$84.00 for a professional — and an external distributor of the same feed pays a four-figure monthly fee plus per-subscriber charges. Same software, same screen, three orders of magnitude apart.

The test is not about skill, and it is broader than incorporation. Employment by a financial firm in a covered role, registration with any regulator or exchange, and use of the data for any business purpose each independently make you professional, regardless of whose money you trade. The incoming CT Plan — replacing the CTA/CQ/UTP plans, expected to launch early 2027 — reportedly makes the test explicitly use-based, capturing any use "by or on behalf of any entity". That wording is reported but not confirmed against the primary order; read it before treating the entitlement design as settled. The knowledge layer

what "I personally update it" means, specified

This is the requirement I have served worst in previous drafts, so it is specified here in full. The ask was not a chatbot. It was a knowledge base that I author and keep current. That is a subsystem with a schema, an authoring loop, a distribution channel, a trust model and an approval gate.

The object: a knowledge pack

A pack is a versioned set of claims. Each claim has a stable identity, a body, a confidence score, a decay date and sources. Stable identity is the whole trick: because a claim persists across versions, the terminal can compute a claim-level diff and show you precisely what changed and why — rather than handing you a new opaque blob and asking you to trust it.

FieldPurpose
idStable across versions, e.g. gold.realyield.decoupled. Never reused for a different assertion.
bodyThe claim itself. One assertion, not a paragraph of hedging.
why_it_mattersWhat it changes about a decision. A claim that changes nothing does not belong in the pack.
confidence0–1. Below 0.6 renders with a visible warning.
decaysThe date after which it must be re-verified. Expired claims render struck-through with a warning, and never silently.
sourcesWhere it came from. A claim with no source cannot be signed.
supersedesOptional — the claim id this replaces, so the lineage is auditable.

Two constraints that shape this, honestly stated

  • A scheduled agent on a consumer plan is capped at single-digit-to-low-tens of runs per day. A "one job per pack class" design across dozens of packs is therefore impossible. The pipeline is one scheduled job that batches many packs per run — which is also where batch pricing lives, so it is cheaper as well as feasible.
  • This lives beside the product, not inside the binary. The authoring pipeline runs on my side against a private repo; the terminal only ever consumes signed output. That is a better design anyway — the signing key never ships to you — but it does mean the update loop is a service you rely on, not a feature you own outright. If that pipeline stops, the terminal keeps working with the last verified packs and tells you they are ageing.

Retrieval over your own corpus

  • Structure-aware chunking of filings by Item, so a citation resolves to a section rather than an arbitrary window.
  • XBRL facts in preference to prose wherever a number exists in both. Never ask a model to read a figure it could look up.
  • Hybrid retrieval — BM25 plus dense — with reranking, because filing language is formulaic and lexical match matters.
  • Corpus capped to the watchlist (~200k chunks). Embedding everything is ~4.7M chunks and turns a sub-100ms lookup into a multi-second scan.
  • Freshness weighting, so the current 10-K outranks the 2019 one unless you asked historically.
  • Citation granularity to (accession, byte offset, length, hash), with the immutable original retained — which turns a citation chip from a presentation feature into a tamper-evident chain.

DPAPI does not protect you from the actual threat

CryptProtectData binds a secret to your user account — and any process running as you can call CryptUnprotectData. That is precisely what the current infostealer families do. Chrome's App-Bound Encryption tried to fix this class of problem with an elevation-service identity check and was bypassed in about 45 days, then broken structurally.

What raises the bar: CNG with VBS/Credential-Guard-class key isolation, or the TPM platform provider, plus a user passphrase (Argon2id) so the ciphertext is not silently machine-recoverable. SQLCipher, EFS and BitLocker protect against a stolen laptop, not against malware running as you.

Be precise about what that buys, because it is narrower than it looks. VBS isolation stops the key being stolen; it does not stop it being used. A CNG persisted key is protected by a container ACL naming the creating user, so malware as you can ask the key to decrypt and read plaintext out of the oracle. The real gain is defeating offline and passive file theft and forcing the attacker to stay resident — which only helps if something detects residency. Plan the detection, or do not claim the benefit.

The exploit chain adversarial review found

The natural-language screener compiles model output into SQL and runs it against DuckDB. Chain that to untrusted content and you get injected filing text → model-authored query → database engine. DuckDB by default can INSTALL and LOAD extensions, read http:// URLs, and COPY TO a UNC path. That is arbitrary read and exfiltration from a hostile footnote, through a feature that looks like a safety property because "the model never produces the rows".

The fix is cheap and must be in from the start: generated SQL runs on a separate read-only connection with extension loading, external filesystem and network access disabled, an allow-list of queryable views rather than raw tables, a statement timeout, and a parsed-AST check rejecting anything that is not a single SELECT. Compiling to a restricted query AST rather than to SQL text is better still.

Anti-patterns explicitly rejected

  • DPAPI alone for secrets, presented as protection. It is not, against the realistic adversary.
  • A "not financial advice" disclaimer standing in for a design control. Disclaimers do not constrain behaviour; architecture does.
  • Trusting the AI to obey a prompt-level rule that matters. If it matters, it is enforced in code.
  • Blanket telemetry with an opt-out. Local-only by default, explicit opt-in to send.
  • Claiming zero data retention the account tier does not actually provide.
  • Storing the pack signing key anywhere an automated agent can reach it.

Regulatory perimeter

So long as it is your money and your eyes only, the advice perimeter does not bite — you cannot advise yourself, so the Advisers Act, FCA Article 53, the financial promotions regime, MiFID II advice and MAR Art.20 recommendation-disclosure are all inapplicable. The AI can therefore be as opinionated and directive as you want it to be, and this plan assumes it should be.

But the trigger is a second person's money or eyes, not a second install. A family member's account, an informal partnership, a fund seeded later, or forwarding the AI's output to a friend each cross it while the software remains a single seat. Earlier drafts of this document got that wrong.

Two exposures the single-user framing does not clear

  • Data protection on third parties. The insider archive and person graph are a profiling database of named natural persons — directors, officers, PDMRs — held for years and enriched with behavioural scoring. UK data protection has a personal-household exemption, but it is read narrowly and a systematic profiling database built for financial gain sits near its edge. Concretely: keep a register of every table holding identified persons, set a retention policy, and do not extend the graph to inferred personal attributes. There is also a sui generis database right question over the archive itself.
  • EU AI Act: out of scope, but only because you are UK-resident. Its transparency duties attach to a provider, and "putting into service" expressly includes supply for one's own use — so an EU-resident building this for himself could be caught at a single user. Revisit if you relocate.

If a second user ever appears, everything changes at once: you become a redistributor, exchange fees multiply per subscriber, vendor agreements and audit clauses attach, the advice perimeter reappears, and you need an EULA, authentication and a support burden. This is why no-distribution is an architectural invariant with a test, and why the MAR wrapper ships dormant rather than absent.

Ctrl+Alt must not be a modifier family. AltGr emits Ctrl+Alt on international layouts, and Microsoft's own guidance is to avoid it. On a UK layout, AltGr+4 (€) would fire a link-channel change. Where density and WCAG genuinely conflict

Both are claimed by most terminals and they do not fully co-exist. WCAG 2.2's target-size criterion effectively requires 24px between the centres of adjacent undersized targets. A 22px compact row and an 18px dense row both fail it; only the 28px comfortable row passes. Two further traps: as-of provenance stamps at 10–12px are normal text under the guidelines, not large text, so they need the full 4.5:1 — which makes the trust layer the thing most likely to fail — and channel hues must not be reused for direction or status.

So: Comfortable is the conformant default, and Compact and Dense are an informed opt-out the settings screen labels as such. Claiming blanket AA while shipping 20px rows is an accessibility statement that is technically a lie.

Accessibility commitments

  • Global Freeze (Alt+P) halts all animation and pins every visible value with its freeze timestamp. WCAG requires a pause mechanism for auto-updating content lasting over five seconds, so this is a requirement, not a nicety — and it is exactly what you want at 09:30 anyway. The copy must not promise buffered replay: dropped updates are dropped, and saying otherwise promises data the system discarded.
  • Screen readers get UIA notification events with most-recent processing. Never a live region on the tape, which produces continuous unusable speech.
  • 200% font scaling without layout collapse; reduced-motion honoured throughout.
  • Keyboard-only operation audited as a release gate, not asserted.
  • Colour-blind modes: Okabe-Ito default, plus cyan/magenta and classic red/green, with a display calibration card at onboarding that renders all palettes, link chips at true pixel size, a 4.5:1 reference pair and a flash sample — because WPF provides no colour-managed sRGB pipeline and wide-gamut or HDR displays demonstrably shift these.

The number that matters

96 engineer-weeks to v1 is the irreducible figure — the work assuming nothing goes wrong. Apply an honest solo multiplier of 1.6–2.5×, and v1 is 154–240 engineer-weeks. At a realistic solo throughput of roughly 0.65 productive weeks per calendar week, that is four and a half to seven years. Full scope including phase 7 runs longer.

That is what "do not miss any features out" costs, stated plainly rather than buried. It is not an argument against the scope — it is the information needed to decide about it. Three things make the estimate self-correcting rather than a guess: an effort ledger from week one, so your real multiplier is known after ten features; a feature ceiling, because solo maintenance at 209 features consumes 20–30 weeks a year against a capacity of about 34; and the phase boundaries above, each of which is a defensible place to stop.
