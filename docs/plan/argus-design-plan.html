Windows terminal · FX & gold · information only · plan

Who is positioned, who is hedging their own production, and who is quietly accumulating.

A native Windows research terminal for one person, built around FX and XAUUSD. No order execution, no broker connectivity. Its lens is incentives — but translated out of the equity world into the one you actually trade: cohort positioning, producer hedging, official-sector accumulation and physical flow, sitting on a knowledge base I author, version and sign on a schedule.

Codename Argus Scope single user, UK, non-pro Stack .NET 10 LTS + WPF To MVP 30–48 eng-weeks Data cost ~$0/mo Research 9 dossiers · 4 red teams

Version 0.2 — I had this pointed the wrong way

The previous draft argued FX was the weakest leg of the product and should be demoted to macro context. As an argument about the market that was right — TradingView already owns cheap FX charting. As an argument about your terminal it was wrong, because FX and gold are what you trade. This version restructures around that, and the good news is that it makes the product substantially cheaper and somewhat smaller: almost every data source the gold and FX lens needs is free at origin.

The equity insider engine is not deleted — it is demoted to an optional module with a genuine bridge into gold (miner hedge books and miner insider transactions). See FEAT for how much of it I would still build, and the open question at the end.

BET

The gap this fills

for a gold and FX desk specifically

The gold and FX information landscape is the opposite of the equity one. Equities have expensive, well-integrated terminals. Gold and FX have a dozen free, excellent, completely disconnected dashboards — CFTC's own Socrata endpoints, LBMA vault data, World Gold Council flows, CME warehouse stocks, Swiss customs, the Bank of England's statistical database — plus TradingView for charting and ForexFactory for a calendar. Nothing joins them.

So the gap is not access to data. It is integration, vintage discipline and interpretation. Nobody sells a single surface where producer hedging, bullion-bank positioning, ETF flow, official-sector accumulation, physical premia and the real-yield regime sit together, each stamped with its own staleness, with a curated knowledge layer explaining what the configuration has historically meant.

LEG 1

Integration nobody offers

Ten free sources, ten browser tabs, ten different update cadences and no shared timeline. One desk that joins them is genuinely absent at any price.

LEG 2

Positioning as the insider lens

Gold has real insiders — producers hedging their own output, bullion banks running the book, central banks accumulating. The equity concept translates cleanly and nobody has built it as a lens.

LEG 3

Vintage honesty

COT is three days stale by construction, the Bank Participation Report is monthly, central-bank data runs two months behind. Most gold dashboards render these as if they were live.

LEG 4

Versioned, cited knowledge

A curated, diffable knowledge base — event playbooks, pair dossiers, regime histories — is a different object from a chatbot over your documents.

The single best argument for building this

The standard gold model has broken, and most tools have not noticed. From 2003 to 2022, gold traded inversely to 10-year real yields with a rolling correlation around −0.73 — roughly an 18% fall in the real gold price per 100bp rise in real yields. Since 2024 that has decoupled, because the official sector replaced ETF flows as the marginal buyer. Price has climbed alongside positive real yields.

A terminal that plots gold against TIPS and stops is showing you a broken model with a straight face. The design answer is a driver panel that displays the current correlation next to its historical value, so a regime break is the first thing you see rather than something you infer months late. That is a small design decision with a large payoff, and it is the kind of thing this product should be built to catch.

Why this is not a stretched analogy

Producer hedging is the strongest case. A gold miner selling forward is doing something structurally identical to a CFO selling stock: disposing of an asset they produce, with better information about its economics than anyone else, under a disclosure regime. The same caveat applies too — just as most Form 4 selling is mechanical tax withholding rather than a view, much producer hedging is driven by project-finance covenants rather than a price call. The decoder logic is the same shape: separate the mechanical from the discretionary, and only score the discretionary.

That is the bridge back to equities. Miner hedge-book disclosures and miner insider transactions are gold sentiment data — filed by companies, readable with the Form 4 and filings engine from v0.1. So the equity work is not wasted; it is repointed at roughly forty names that matter to gold rather than at the whole market.

FX, for a UK trader

The same discipline applies with different plumbing. FX has no consolidated tape either, so every quote is labelled with its venue. The positioning lens is CFTC's financial-futures COT (leveraged funds, asset managers, dealers); the driver stack is rate differentials, OIS-implied policy paths and carry computed from free central-bank curves; and for sterling specifically the Bank of England's Interactive Statistical Database serves Bank Rate, SONIA, gilt yields and the effective exchange rate index over a plain CSV endpoint at no cost.

The invariant still holds, and now it costs you nothing

Argus must never become a distributor. That mattered enormously in v0.1 because real-time US equity depth carried exchange fees. In a gold and FX build it barely binds, because the positioning and physical layer is public regulatory data and your price feed is your own broker's. The invariant stays architectural anyway — no network listener, no export of raw licensed ticks, no hosted view — because it costs nothing to keep and everything to retrofit.

SCRN

Mockups

rendered UI · dark by design, on any page theme

These are live HTML, not pictures — the type, density, colour and column structure below are the real specification. They render dark regardless of your page theme because the terminal itself is dark; that is a deliberate single-theme commitment for the app, not an omission. All figures carry a SAMPLE DATA stamp: tickers are real companies, but every filing, price and name is illustrative. Nothing here is a record of an actual transaction by an actual person.

What the mockups are arguing

  • Density is the feature. 20–24px rows, hairline rules, tabular figures, no cards, no shadows. Whitespace in a blotter is paid for in scroll events.
  • Every number carries its own vintage. A 13F row is 45 days stale by law, short interest is bi-monthly, a Form 4 is near-live. The ownership panel never blends them into one "as of" — each row is stamped separately.
  • Fact and inference are different surfaces, not different tones of voice. Facts come from deterministic pipelines with no model in the path. Inference is visually segregated, and any numeric sentence without a citation is struck before it renders.
  • Failure is a designed screen. A dead provider greys its panel and stamps the age of the last good value. It never renders stale data as live, and never silently fails over to another source without saying so.

Module 3 · Equities & insiders OPTIONAL

This was the flagship in v0.1. Given FX and gold are what you trade, it is now optional — but it is not wasted, and I would still build a reduced version for two reasons. First, gold miners are a real sentiment channel: hedge books and insider transactions at forty producers tell you what the people closest to the metal are doing. Second, the filings engine is the same machinery either way.

My recommendation: build the Form 4 decoder, conviction score and cluster radar, but point them at a gold-miner universe rather than the whole market — roughly a third of the original effort for most of the value to you. The full equity module below stays in the plan for reference and can be switched on later; nothing about it is thrown away.

Features a plan like this normally forgets

These are the ones that quietly decide whether the terminal is trustworthy, and every one of them came out of the research rather than out of a feature brainstorm:

  • The 23×5 session engine. The UTP tape moves to 23 hours a day, five days a week on 6 December 2026, Sunday 20:00 ET to Friday 20:00 ET, with new overnight LULD band indicators. Any data model where "trading day" equals "calendar date" is structurally wrong within months of launch. A session must be a named, venue-specific interval.
  • The Form 4 filing wave is 16:00–22:00 ET, not intraday — EDGAR's Section 16 window extends to 22:00. An insider alert designed around market hours will miss almost everything.
  • FIGI-keyed identity. Tickers are mutable display labels. Reuse, spin-offs, mergers and delistings all corrupt a ticker-keyed store.
  • Survivorship. Every historical screen must run against a point-in-time universe including dead names, or every backward-looking number is flattering nonsense.
  • Adjustment policy as an explicit switch — unadjusted vs split vs split-and-dividend. The classic bug is adjusting an already-adjusted series.
  • Q4 is never filed and cash-flow facts are year-to-date cumulative, so TTM must be derived by differencing. XBRL also carries no statement structure — you cannot render a financial statement from companyfacts alone.
  • $TICK, $TRIN and $ADD are proprietary calculated indices. They cannot be computed from a consolidated tape; they are a paid feed or they are absent.
  • Retail FX "volume" is tick count. Showing it as volume is a lie the UI should refuse to tell.

A correctness inversion worth fixing before it costs money

The distress-sale detector

The conviction model down-weights routine selling, the default filters hide it, and sales render in a low-contrast neutral. Those three sensible decisions compose into one bad outcome: the terminal systematically mutes the single event a research tool exists to catch — a large, discretionary, off-plan insider sale immediately before bad news. Sales need a separate signal path that bypasses the routine down-weight entirely: no 10b5-1 plan, size above a holding or salary threshold, unusual timing.

Workspaces and lifecycle states that were missing

  • The Book — a live P&L blotter with day and open P&L, R-multiple to date, % of book, days held, next catalyst. The single most-opened screen for anyone with money at risk, and it was not among the six shipped workspaces.
  • Earnings Day — T-5 prep card, T-0 automatic 8-K Ex-99.1 extraction with a headline-vs-consensus diff inside 60 seconds, guidance language diff, live call panel. Named in the roadmap, absent from the workspace set.
  • Weekend Desk — gap-risk table with historical weekend gap distributions, and the Sunday 17:00 ET FX open with an explicit thin-liquidity state.
  • Delisting to Chapter 11. Halt → suspension → Form 25 → Form 15 → bankruptcy. At Form 15 the filings stop permanently, and the draft would have rendered a dead company as a coverage gap rather than a terminal state.
  • The closing auction, and returning after two weeks away — a "what did I miss" digest rather than an unreadable backlog.

A claim in my draft that was wrong

I priced the incentives pillar at $0 including the non-US regimes. That is only true for the US and the EU national registers. UK regulatory news is distributed via RNS, a commercial LSE feed (the FCA's National Storage Mechanism is free but is an unstructured document store); Japan's EDINET requires a registered API key and returns Japanese-language XBRL; Canada's SEDI has no documented bulk API; India's disclosures land as per-exchange HTML and PDF. Read that row as "free at origin for US and EU; commercial or scrape-only elsewhere".

Related: companyfacts is us-gaap only and starts around 2010. A "global equities" fundamentals claim needs a separate IFRS/ESEF, EDINET and SEDAR+ path — and a 20-year owner-earnings series needs a source the plan has not named.

Non-goals

Deliberately excluded, each for a reason: order execution and broker connectivity (a different regulatory and security object entirely); anything multi-user — sharing, hosted views, publishing (triggers the redistribution cliff); signals-as-a-service or copy-trading; social features; alt data with PII (credit-card panels, geolocation — MNPI and privacy hazards for no edge you can act on); automated strategy backtesting as a product (the read-only evidence panels cover the honest 80% without pretending to a research platform).

Time-sensitive facts a naive gold or FX plan gets wrong

  • There is no consolidated tape for gold or FX. The XAUUSD number on a retail platform is a dealer quote or an indicative aggregated mid, not a market price. The only executable gold benchmarks are the two daily LBMA auctions, administered by ICE Benchmark Administration at 10:30 and 15:00 London.
  • GOFO was discontinued in January 2015. Gold lease rates are no longer published and must be derived from SOFR less the forward-implied rate. Any source offering a live GOFO feed is offering a reconstruction — which is fine, provided it says so.
  • COT is three days stale by construction — Tuesday's positions, released Friday at 15:30 ET. The Bank Participation Report is monthly (first-Tuesday data, first-Friday release). Central-bank reserve data runs roughly two months behind. None of these are timing tools and a terminal that renders them as current is lying by omission.
  • The gold–real yield relationship has broken. Roughly −0.73 from 2003 to 2022; decoupled since 2024 as the official sector displaced ETFs as marginal buyer. Any model, dashboard or knowledge pack still asserting the old relationship as a rule is wrong right now.
  • Retail FX volume is tick count, not volume. Displaying it as volume is a lie the UI should refuse to tell.
  • Swiss export data distinguishes consumption from vault rebuild. Metal moving to China or India is demand; metal moving to the UK is London vault replenishment. Aggregating them destroys the signal.
  • China's import pipe is licensed and rationed, so the Shanghai premium measures demand pressing against fixed-width supply rather than demand alone — and metal generally cannot flow back out.
  • FX week boundary is Sunday 17:00 ET, with DST transitions on both sides of a pair and spread widening at rollover. Gold trades nearly around the clock but has its own session rhythm keyed to the London auctions and COMEX pit hours.
AI

The knowledge layer

what "I update it personally" actually means

You asked me to keep the AI knowledge current myself. The honest mechanism, and its one real constraint, are worth stating plainly.

The constraint

A scheduled Claude Code Routine — the natural way to run "Claude updates this nightly" — requires a claude.ai subscription login and cannot be driven by an API key. So this cannot be a subsystem that ships inside the binary and runs on its own. It has to be your pipeline: a Routine on your account, operating on a private repo, publishing signed packs the terminal then pulls. That is a better design anyway — the signing key never ships in the app — but it does mean the update loop lives beside the product rather than inside it.

There is a second constraint that kills the obvious design. Routines are capped at single-digit-to-low-tens of runs per day depending on plan. A "one Routine per pack class" architecture across hundreds of packs is therefore impossible. The pipeline has to be a plain scheduled job (GitHub Action or equivalent) calling the Batch API, which is where the batch discount lives anyway. One job, many packs per run, batched overnight.

Knowledge Packs

A pack is a versioned set of claims. Each claim carries a stable ID, a body, sources, a confidence score and a decay date. Stable IDs are what make the diff possible: because a claim persists across versions, the terminal can show you exactly which claims were added, revised or retired last night, and why — the panel in screen 03.

SEC

Security

two real adversaries, honestly assessed

Single-user removes multi-tenancy, auth and account-takeover from the threat model entirely. What remains is worth taking seriously, and one common mitigation is largely theatre.

DPAPI does not protect you from the actual threat

CryptProtectData binds a secret to your user account — but any process running as you can call CryptUnprotectData. That is precisely what the current infostealer families do. Chrome's App-Bound Encryption tried to fix this class of problem with an elevation-service identity check and was bypassed in about 45 days, then broken structurally.

What raises the bar: CNG with VBS/Credential-Guard-class key isolation, or the TPM platform crypto provider, plus a user passphrase (Argon2id) so the ciphertext is not silently machine-recoverable. SQLCipher, EFS and BitLocker protect against a stolen laptop — not against malware running as you.

Be precise about what that buys, because it is narrower than it first appears. VBS isolation stops the key being stolen; it does not stop the key being used. A CNG persisted key is protected by a container ACL that by default names the creating user, so malware running as you can simply ask the key to decrypt and read the plaintext out of the oracle. The real gain is that it defeats offline and passive file theft and forces the attacker to stay resident on the box — which is only an advantage if something detects residency. Plan the detection, or do not claim the benefit.

Worth knowing: trading brands are actively impersonated. One vendor tracked 75+ malicious "TradingView Premium" ad campaigns across 500+ domains. Your own download path is an attack surface, which is the real argument for signing the build.

The LLM boundary

This is the largest new attack surface, and it is not hypothetical. SEC filings, RNS announcements, news, PDFs and RSS are attacker-controlled text. A hostile footnote in an 8-K is indirect prompt injection with a direct payoff: combined with markdown image rendering in a WebView2 panel, it can exfiltrate your watchlist — which is your alpha — through an image URL.

The exploit chain I had left open

The natural-language screener compiles model output into SQL and runs it against DuckDB. Chain that to the untrusted-content problem and you get: injected filing text → model-authored query → database engine. DuckDB by default can INSTALL and LOAD extensions, read http:// URLs, and COPY TO a UNC path. That is arbitrary read and exfiltration from a hostile footnote, straight through a feature I had listed as a safety property because "the model never produces the rows".

The fix is cheap and must be in from the start: run all generated SQL on a separate read-only connection with extension loading, external filesystem and network access disabled, an allow-list of queryable views rather than raw tables, a statement timeout, and a parsed-AST check that rejects anything that is not a single SELECT. Compiling to a restricted query AST rather than to raw SQL text is better still.

Controls I had missing entirely

  • Assume-breach runbook. The security posture says assume compromise and optimise for cheap recovery, then never writes the recovery. One action to revoke every vendor key, rotate the pack trust root, and rebuild from a known-good backup — written down and rehearsed.
  • Backup and escrow. The design seals your only irreplaceable data — journal, theses, watchlists — behind a hardware-bound key. A TPM clear or a dead motherboard destroys it. Scheduled encrypted backup under a passphrase-only KDF, plus a printed recovery code, with a restore actually tested rather than assumed.
  • Host preconditions gate. Reporting "VBS — best protection" on an unencrypted disk is a false assurance. Check and display the real state: disk encryption, VBS availability, TPM presence, whether you are running as admin.
  • Adversarial eval suite, separate from the correctness golden set. Regenerated poisoned filings and hidden-instruction fixtures per run. A correctness eval detects regressions; it does not detect attacks.
  • Model-deprecation runbook. The build window is years; models deprecate faster. Pin model IDs in config, keep an inventory of every behaviour that is model-specific, and gate swaps behind a re-eval. Do not make any single model's behaviour a load-bearing security invariant.
  • CI trust root. If an autonomous agent has write access to the pack repo, it can commit a workflow file and tag it — and a tag-triggered workflow runs with the signing secret. "The agent commits, only CI signs" is false unless workflow files are protected and tag triggers are restricted to a branch the agent cannot write.

Regulatory perimeter — corrected

My first pass framed this as "the perimeter switches on when a second user appears". That is wrong, and the red team was right to catch it. The trigger for the Advisers Act, FCA Article 53 and MiFID II is advising on or managing another person's money, or communicating a recommendation to another person — which can happen with zero additional installs. A family member's account, an informal partnership, a fund seeded later, or forwarding the AI's output to a friend all cross it while the software remains a single-seat install.

Stated correctly: so long as it is your money and your eyes only, the advice perimeter does not bite and the AI can be as opinionated and directive as you want. The binding constraint is the market-data licensing cliff, which is why no-distribution is architectural. Keep the MAR Art.20 disclosure wrapper shipped-but-dormant so the compliance shape exists if that ever changes.

Two exposures the single-user framing does not clear

  • Data protection on third-party individuals. This is the regime that genuinely applies to you, and I missed it entirely first time. The insider archive and person graph are a profiling database of named natural persons — directors, officers, PDMRs — held for years and enriched with behavioural scoring. UK/EU data protection has a personal-household exemption, but it is read narrowly and a systematic profiling database built for financial gain sits close to its edge. Concretely: keep a register of every table holding identified persons, set a retention policy, and do not extend the graph to inferred personal attributes. There is also a sui generis database right question over the archive itself.
  • EU AI Act: resolved, does not apply. You are UK-resident, so the Act's transparency duties are out of scope. Worth recording why the question was asked: those duties attach to a provider, and "putting into service" includes supply for your own use, so an EU-resident building this for himself could have been caught at a single user. Revisit only if you relocate.

A binding bug in my first draft

I originally bound link channels to Ctrl+Alt+1..6 and provenance mode to Ctrl+/. Both are wrong on a UK keyboard, which is presumably yours. Three interaction modes the draft was missing

  • Fast Market Mode. The design optimises for sustained reading and never for peak cognitive load. One key collapses to three pinned panels — chart, tape, alerts — suspends every inference surface, kills animation, and widens rows. Density and fourteen semantic hues are actively counterproductive in the ninety seconds that decide an outcome.
  • Global undo. A keyboard-first design with single-key destructive verbs and no undo is a trap. One bus covering watchlist edits, alert deletion, layout resets and journal merges.
  • Trust incident log. Flag any number as "looks wrong" and capture its value, full provenance chain and panel command into a local list. The product's central claim is auditability; this is the only feedback loop that tests whether the provenance design actually works. Every entry is either a data bug or a design bug.
  • "Why not?" alert tracer. The plan argues an alert with an invisible evaluation clock is undebuggable, then ships only the firing case. The non-firing case is what destroys trust.

Freeze is mandatory, not a nicety

WCAG 2.2 caps flashing at three per second, and requires a pause mechanism for auto-updating content lasting more than five seconds. A live tape violates both by default. So: a global Freeze key that halts all animation and pins every visible value with its freeze timestamp, and a flash governor that degrades from per-cell flashing to a steady tint above three updates per second. Both are accessibility requirements that happen to be exactly what you want at 09:30 anyway. Screen readers get UIA notification events with most-recent processing — never a live region on the tape, which would produce continuous unusable speech.

Where this estimate will be wrong

Refocusing on gold and FX cut real scope, not just presentation: no XBRL financial model, no equity symbology and corporate-actions layer, no consensus estimates, no market-wide universe. Those were four of the five things most likely to overrun. The v1 figure drops from ~66 to ~58 irreducible weeks, and the risky portion of it shrinks considerably.

The feasibility critic went at a fuller version of the old scope and returned 380–520 engineer-weeks, arguing that at a realistic solo throughput of about 0.65 productive weeks per calendar week that is over a decade.

That number is for a considerably larger feature set than the one above, so I do not accept it directly. But the direction is right and I was too optimistic. Treat the table as ~66 weeks of irreducible work and assume a 1.6–2.5× multiplier until you have measured your own — so roughly 93–145 weeks to v1, with the MVP at 30 weeks becoming 48–75. The modules that habitually run 3–5× over are, in order: charting (the last 10% of interaction polish is half the work), real-time infrastructure (reconnect, gap-fill, backpressure), and the release-calendar scheduler, which looks trivial and is not — DST, holidays, delayed publications and revised vintages all land here.

Three cheap mechanisms make this self-correcting rather than a guess: an effort ledger recording estimated versus actual per feature, so your real multiplier is known after about ten features; a feature ceiling with a displacement rule — past roughly 120 shipped features, solo maintenance consumes the entire year, so every addition must retire something; and a hardware line in the budget, which the cost table omits entirely despite committing to GPU chart panes and a large local lake.

What I would cut first

If this has to be smaller: drop the equity module entirely, drop silver, drop the vol surface, drop chart replay. The minimum slice that still delivers the differentiating value is Phase 0 plus Phase 1 — about 18 weeks — because an integrated positioning, physical and regime desk for gold is already something nobody sells, and it runs on free data.

That is a meaningfully better position than v0.1 offered. The old minimum slice was 26 weeks and carried a real monthly data bill before it showed you anything. This one is shorter, cheaper, and the thing it proves first is the thing you would actually use every day.
