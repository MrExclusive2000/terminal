Who is buying it with their own money?

The flagship, and the reason the rest exists. Everything here turns on one distinction: most Form 4 activity is payroll, not opinion. Measured on a recent quarter, 67% of rows are non-discretionary — awards, tax withholding, option mechanics — and open-market purchases are just 5.5%, about 22,700 a year across the whole market. A product that shows you "insider activity" without decoding the code is showing you a company's HR department.

The second distinction is who. A director who buys every January carries no information; the filer who has never bought in this month in three years and just did, carries most of it. That split is the single most valuable transform in this plan.

Who owns it, and who is short it?

Ownership data is a minefield of vintages. A 13F is 45 days late by law and can describe a position 135 days old; it shows longs only, no shorts, no derivatives. Shown honestly it is useful context. Shown as though it were current it is a liability.

The short side has a quiet scandal in it: the US number is bi-monthly and aggregate, while the FCA and EU registers name the actual holder and update daily. Free, better, and almost nobody outside institutions uses it.

Is the company running out of money?

In the small-cap universe where insider clusters actually appear, issuance is the dominant loss mechanism — not being wrong about the business. A company with an open shelf, a cash runway under eight quarters and language quietly dropped from its risk factors will sell stock into any strength you create. This module exists to make you check that before you get excited about a cluster buy.

The valuation work is deliberately Buffett-school: owner earnings with maintenance capex stated rather than buried, reverse DCF so the market's assumption is explicit, and a capital-allocation record over a decade. Multiples are a comparison tool, not an answer.

Who is positioned, and how crowded?

Cohort positioning is the closest thing FX and metals have to insider filings, and the mapping is closer than it looks: a miner selling forward production is disposing of an asset they produce with better knowledge of its economics than anyone else — structurally the same act as a CFO selling stock.

The same caveat applies too. Much producer hedging is project-finance covenant, not a price view, exactly as most Form 4 selling is tax withholding. One decoder, two asset classes: separate the mechanical from the discretionary and only score the discretionary.

And the honest framing: positioning is fuel, not a trigger. Crowded gets more crowded.

Where is the metal, and who is accumulating it?

Gold has no consolidated tape, so the first job is refusing to pretend otherwise: your IC Markets XAUUSD is a CFD dealer quote, and the only executable benchmarks are the two daily LBMA auctions. The panel shows four venues and the basis between them.

The second job is the regime. Gold traded inversely to real yields at about −0.73 for two decades and then stopped, once central banks displaced ETFs as the marginal buyer. A terminal that plots gold against TIPS and stops is showing you a dead model with a straight face. Every correlation here is displayed beside its long-run value.

How do I find it, and how do I get told?

The screener compiles natural language into a query you can read and edit. The model writes the query; the query produces the rows. Same screen twice gives the same answer.

Two alerting details that most products get wrong: Form 4 filings land 16:00–22:00 ET, not intraday, so an insider alert built around market hours misses nearly everything. And the system shows you why a rule did not fire — the silent non-firing case is what destroys trust in an alert engine.

What should I size this at?

Given an entry, a stop and a risk tolerance, the size is determined — it is arithmetic, and it is arithmetic people do badly under pressure. The traps are real: contract sizes differ (100oz a lot on gold, 100,000 units on FX majors), P&L lands in the quote currency so a sterling account needs a conversion on every instrument, and broker volume steps must be floored so realised risk lands under target rather than over.

What does my own record say?

The panel that tells you about you, and the one that looks least impressive. Most discretionary traders do not lose for want of data; they lose to sizing drift, overtrading and holding through events.

Design constraint learned the hard way: discipline features that lecture get switched off within a week, taking the journal with them. Capture in under six seconds, statistics that are genuinely interesting, and no adjectives. "Your last four trades after a losing day averaged −0.8R" is useful. "Consider whether you are revenge trading" is how you get uninstalled.
