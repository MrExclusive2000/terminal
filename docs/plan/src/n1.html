JOB

What this is actually for

stated the way I'd state it to a desk

Most trading software answers the wrong question. It tells you what the price did, in more colours and on more timeframes than anyone needs. Price is the most crowded data in the industry and the least differentiated — everybody has it, it's free, and it's already in everybody else's model.

The question worth asking is different: who knows more than me, and what are they doing about it? That question has a good answer, and it's a boring one. The people who know more are frequently compelled to tell you. Not out of generosity — because regulators force them.

The through-line: compelled disclosure

A CFO buying her own stock has to file within two business days. A fund crossing 5% has to file within five. A miner hedging next year's production shows up in a positioning report every Friday. A central bank buying gold reports it to the IMF. A director pledging shares to a bank discloses it in the proxy. A company must publish which metric its CEO is paid to hit.

None of these people want to tell you. All of them have to. That obligation trail is the one dataset in finance where the informed participant is legally required to show their hand, and it is almost entirely free at origin. Everything in this plan is built to collect it, normalise it, and put it next to the price.

The second thing, which matters more than people admit

Compelled disclosure tells you what informed people chose to do. The higher-quality signal is what people are forced to do. Forced flow doesn't care about your thesis and doesn't change its mind:

Every one of these is knowable in advance. None of them requires a prediction. This is the part of the plan I'd defend hardest, because it's the part that doesn't depend on being cleverer than the market — only on being better organised than the people who haven't bothered to look.

What I'm not building, and why

I've watched a lot of terminals get built. They fail the same way: they add surface until nothing is trustworthy, and the honest answer to "where did this number come from?" becomes "somewhere".

  • No execution, no broker connection. You trade in MT5. This informs, it doesn't act. That's a smaller regulatory object, a smaller security object, and a smaller failure mode.
  • No prediction engine. No "AI price target". If I can't show you the evidence and the thing that would falsify it, I'd rather show you nothing.
  • No second user, ever. The moment someone else sees the screen, this stops being a subscriber and becomes a distributor. Same feed, three orders of magnitude in cost. It's an architectural invariant with a test, not a note in a README.
  • No plugins, no third-party extensions. The AI reads attacker-controlled text — filings and news. Every extension point is a hole in the only boundary keeping that safe.
  • No fake numbers to look complete. Retail FX "volume" is a tick count. If I don't have volume, the panel says so.

Where the edge is, and where it isn't

the part most plans skip

A plan that only lists what works is marketing. Here's my honest read, from both sides.

Insider buying carries informationHolds up. Open-market purchases by officers, clustered, off-plan, are the best-documented anomaly in this space. Cohen–Malloy–Pomorski's split matters: opportunistic filers carry the return, routine filers carry roughly none.Scored, not just listed. The classifier is the product.

The thing that actually loses money

Not direction. In twenty years of watching people blow up, almost none of it was "they were wrong about the trade". It was size, stops inside the noise, holding through an event they knew was coming, and trading bigger after a loss.

So the terminal computes what most tools leave to your head: position size from your actual stop distance, whether that stop sits inside normal volatility, what the spread costs as a percentage of the risk you're taking, and what your own record says about this setup at this time of day. That's not a discipline lecture — it's arithmetic you'd otherwise do badly under pressure.
