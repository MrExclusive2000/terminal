LOOK

How it looks, and why

density, provenance, and honest accessibility

Financial UI is where designers who've never used the thing do the most damage. The instinct is to make it calmer, lighter, more spacious — and every one of those moves costs the user information per glance. What follows are the principles I'd hold the build to, each with the consequence that falls out of it, because a principle without a consequence is a poster.

Twelve principles, and what each one forces

PrincipleTherefore
Density is the feature20–24px rows, hairline rules, no cards, no shadows, three density modes. Whitespace in a blotter is paid for in scroll events. Pros want density; "clean" is a failure mode here, and the people who ask for it are rarely the people using it at 09:30.
Every number carries provenanceF9 toggles a mode where every figure gains a dotted underline and becomes click-through to its source filing, with an as-of stamp on hover. If a number can't answer "where did you come from?", it shouldn't be on screen.
Staleness is never hiddenPer-row vintage badges. A 45-day-old 13F and a live Form 4 never share one "as of" header. This is the single decision that most separates a terminal you trust from one that's merely fast.
Fact and inference are different surfacesSeparate chrome, marked at glyph level rather than by background tint — users habituate to background colour inside a week, and tint survives neither a screenshot nor the clipboard.
Object first, then verbCRSP US Equity OWN — the Bloomberg grammar, and it endures because it matches how you actually think: name the thing, then ask the question.
No mouse requiredFull keyboard map, focus rings that survive a dark theme, disciplined tab order, and a no-mouse audit as a release gate rather than an aspiration.
Degradation is designedEvery panel specifies its empty, loading, stale, unentitled and provider-down state. A dead provider greys and stamps an age. It never renders stale as live.
The app never lies to look completeNo FX "volume" invented from tick counts. No blended vintages. No silent provider substitution. No invented level. An honest gap builds more trust than a plausible fill.
Peak load is a different modeFast Market Mode collapses to three panels, suspends inference surfaces and kills animation. Density and fourteen semantic hues are actively counterproductive in the ninety seconds that decide an outcome.
Destructive actions are reversibleA global undo bus covering watchlist edits, alert deletion, layout resets and journal merges. A keyboard-first design with single-key verbs and no undo is a trap you build for yourself.
Discipline features earn their placeCapture in under six seconds, statistics that are genuinely interesting, and no adjectives. "Your last four trades after a losing day averaged −0.8R" is useful. "Consider whether you are revenge trading" gets the feature switched off within a week — and takes the journal with it.
The tool is auditable by its userCtrl+Shift+X flags any number as wrong, capturing its full provenance chain. Every entry is either a data bug or a design bug, and it's the only feedback loop that actually tests whether the provenance design works.

Workspace model

Docked tiles with arbitrary splits, true tear-off to a native floating window on another monitor at a different DPI, and full dock-tree persistence — panel identity, symbol binding, chart timeframe, indicator state and scroll position. Six colour-coded linking channels: change the symbol in one panel and every panel on that channel follows. Channel hues are reserved and never reused for direction, selection or status; that constraint is trivially easy to violate by accident and immediately confusing when you do.

Command language

A mnemonic command line as primary, a fuzzy palette as the discoverability escape hatch. Sigils namespace the palette: none for symbols, > for verbs, @ for function mnemonics, # for screens, ? to ask. Discoverability is handled by an always-available catalogue, inline hints as you type, and did-you-mean on a near miss. Fuzzy match never auto-executes on ambiguity — in a fast market, a fat-finger firing the wrong command is far worse than one more keystroke.

Two bindings that look obvious and are wrong

Ctrl+Alt must not be a modifier family. AltGr emits Ctrl+Alt on international layouts, and Microsoft's own guidance is to avoid it. On your UK layout, AltGr+4 (the € key) would fire a link-channel change — a bug that presents as "the app randomly changes my symbols" and takes a fortnight to attribute. Ctrl+Space is the IME toggle on CJK layouts, so the most obvious binding for a command line is the one that breaks it. Both are avoided above, and a keyboard layout guard detects the active layout at first run and on change, warning about collisions. Roughly twenty lines of code to prevent a silent degradation that produces no error message.

Design system

Red/green fails for roughly 1 in 12 men. So the default directional palette is Okabe-Ito blue and vermilion, with classic red/green available as an opt-in rather than the other way round. Direction is additionally encoded by glyph and by sign, so colour is never the sole carrier of the most important thing on the screen.

#0072B2up · Okabe-Ito blue
#D55E00down · vermilion
#35B5A8accent · interactive
#E0A44Bstale / warning
#B49BE8AI inference
#0C1013ground · never pure black

The accent sits in a different hue family from both directional colours, so "this is interactive" can never be misread as "this went up". Contrast tables are generated from the tokens at build time, never hand-written — adversarial review found a hand-written table in an earlier draft that was wrong in both directions, which is what happens when nobody actually runs the numbers.

Typography: IBM Plex Sans Condensed for panel headers, because condensed reads as terminal chrome and buys back column width; IBM Plex Sans for prose; IBM Plex Mono for all data, with tabular figures everywhere digits need to align. FX prices use three-tier rendering — big figure de-emphasised, pip digits large, pipette small — because that's how the number is actually read by someone who trades it. Uptick/downtick flashing is capped at three per second and degrades to a steady tint above that rate.

Where density and WCAG genuinely conflict — and I won't paper over it

Most terminals claim both. They don't fully co-exist. WCAG 2.2's target-size criterion effectively requires 24px between the centres of adjacent undersized targets. A 22px compact row and an 18px dense row both fail it; only the 28px comfortable row passes. Two further traps worth naming: as-of provenance stamps at 10–12px count as normal text under the guidelines rather than large text, so they need the full 4.5:1 — which makes the trust layer the thing most likely to fail the audit — and channel hues must not be reused for direction or status.

So the honest resolution is: Comfortable is the conformant default, and Compact and Dense are an informed opt-out that the settings screen labels as exactly that. Claiming blanket AA while shipping 20px rows is an accessibility statement that is technically a lie, and I'd rather ship a true narrow claim than a false broad one.

Accessibility commitments

  • Global Freeze (Alt+P) halts all animation and pins every visible value with its freeze timestamp. WCAG requires a pause mechanism for auto-updating content lasting over five seconds, so this is a requirement rather than a nicety — and it happens to be exactly what you want at 09:30 anyway. The copy must not promise buffered replay: dropped updates are dropped, and saying otherwise promises data the system threw away.
  • Screen readers get UIA notification events with most-recent processing. Never a live region on the tape, which produces continuous unusable speech.
  • 200% font scaling without layout collapse; reduced-motion honoured throughout.
  • Keyboard-only operation audited as a release gate, not asserted in a document.
  • Colour-blind modes: Okabe-Ito default, plus cyan/magenta and classic red/green, with a display calibration card at onboarding that renders all palettes, link chips at true pixel size, a 4.5:1 reference pair and a flash sample — because WPF provides no colour-managed sRGB pipeline, and wide-gamut or HDR displays demonstrably shift these.

Onboarding and the error states nobody designs

First run asks only the questions that actually change behaviour — trading entity and employment status (which sets your entitlements and therefore your data bill), account currency, instruments — then loads a curated starter workspace rather than an empty grid. Progressive disclosure after that: the catalogue and cheat sheet are always one key away.

The error states below are where most terminals reveal how much they respect the user. A bare dash is a designer declining to answer a reasonable question.
