The screens

nine · live HTML, not pictures

I think in screens, so this is where the plan gets concrete. These are rendered HTML — the density, type, colour and column structure below are the specification. Every figure is stamped SAMPLE DATA: real tickers, illustrative everything else. Nothing here records an actual transaction by an actual person.

What I am arguing with these

  • Density is not a failure of design. 20–24px rows, hairlines, tabular figures, no cards, no drop shadows. Whitespace in a blotter is paid for in scroll events, and a trader scanning forty rows does not want them to breathe.
  • Every number carries its own age. A 13F row is 45 days stale by law; a Form 4 is near-live. They never share an "as of". This is the single decision that most separates a terminal you trust from one that is merely fast.
  • Fact and inference are different surfaces, marked at glyph level — not by background tint, which people stop seeing within a week and which survives neither screenshot nor clipboard.
  • Failure is a designed screen. A dead feed greys its panel and stamps the age of the last good value. It never renders stale as live, and never silently swaps to another source.
  • Both asset classes carry full weight. Screen 01 is equities, FX and metals side by side, each with the lens that applies to it.

The knowledge layer

what "you personally update it" actually requires

Every product is bolting a chatbot onto its documents and calling it AI. That is worth very little: it retrieves what you already had, in a worse format, with a confidence it has not earned.

What is actually useful on a desk is the thing a good analyst builds over years and then takes with them when they leave — accumulated judgement. Which metric matters for a miner and why it differs for a bank. What the last three regime breaks looked like. Which insider patterns have historically meant something and which are payroll. What is typically priced into an NFP print. That is what separates ten years on a desk from a terminal licence.

So this is not a chatbot. It is a maintained body of claims that I write, version, sign and keep current, which the terminal loads and reasons from. It needs a schema, an authoring loop, a distribution channel, a trust model and an approval gate — anything less is a text file that goes stale without telling you.

The object: a knowledge pack

A pack is a versioned set of claims. Each claim has a stable identity, a body, a confidence score, a decay date and sources. Stable identity is the whole trick: because a claim persists across versions, the terminal can compute a claim-level diff and show you precisely what changed and why — rather than handing you a new opaque blob and asking you to trust it.

FieldPurpose
idStable across versions, e.g. gold.realyield.decoupled. Never reused for a different assertion.
bodyThe claim itself. One assertion, not a paragraph of hedging.
why_it_mattersWhat it changes about a decision. A claim that changes nothing does not belong in the pack.
confidence0–1. Below 0.6 renders with a visible warning.
decaysThe date after which it must be re-verified. Expired claims render struck-through with a warning, and never silently.
sourcesWhere it came from. A claim with no source cannot be signed.
supersedesOptional — the claim id this replaces, so the lineage is auditable.

The authoring loop — how I actually update it

StageWhat happensControl
1 · ScheduleA scheduled job runs off-peak against a private repo. Pack classes are due on their own cadence, so not everything regenerates nightly.cron
2 · ResearchEach due pack is researched against its declared sources, with the prior version supplied so the model is revising rather than rewriting.web + local corpus
3 · AuthorClaims written with ids preserved where the assertion persists, superseded where it changed, retired where it no longer holds.schema-validated
4 · Self-checkEvery claim must carry a source. Numeric claims are checked against the cited source. Unsourced claims are dropped, not softened.automated
5 · GateA frozen golden question set (200+ typed questions with known answers) runs against the candidate. Regressions block publication.CI gate
6 · Adversarial gateA separate suite from the correctness set: regenerated poisoned filings, hidden-instruction fixtures, injection canaries. Eval sets catch regressions; they do not catch attacks.CI gate
7 · SignEd25519 over each pack and the index. The key lives in CI, never in the binary, and never in the repo the authoring agent can write to.key custody
8 · DistributeThe terminal polls a small signed index.json with ETag and downloads only changed packs.bandwidth-cheap
9 · VerifySignature checked before load. Failure keeps the previous version and raises a visible alert — it fails closed.hard fail
10 · ReviewPer pack class: auto, review-before-live, or pinned. The claim diff is what you review, not the file.your call
11 · RollbackPrevious versions retained on disk; revert is one click and takes effect without a restart.one click

Two constraints that shape this, honestly stated

  • A scheduled agent on a consumer plan is capped at single-digit-to-low-tens of runs per day. A "one job per pack class" design across dozens of packs is therefore impossible. The pipeline is one scheduled job that batches many packs per run — which is also where batch pricing lives, so it is cheaper as well as feasible.
  • This lives beside the product, not inside the binary. The authoring pipeline runs on my side against a private repo; the terminal only ever consumes signed output. That is a better design anyway — the signing key never ships to you — but it does mean the update loop is a service you rely on, not a feature you own outright. If that pipeline stops, the terminal keeps working with the last verified packs and tells you they are ageing.

Retrieval over your own corpus

  • Structure-aware chunking of filings by Item, so a citation resolves to a section rather than an arbitrary window.
  • XBRL facts in preference to prose wherever a number exists in both. Never ask a model to read a figure it could look up.
  • Hybrid retrieval — BM25 plus dense — with reranking, because filing language is formulaic and lexical match matters.
  • Corpus capped to the watchlist (~200k chunks). Embedding everything is ~4.7M chunks and turns a sub-100ms lookup into a multi-second scan.
  • Freshness weighting, so the current 10-K outranks the 2019 one unless you asked historically.
  • Citation granularity to (accession, byte offset, length, hash), with the immutable original retained — which turns a citation chip from a presentation feature into a tamper-evident chain.

Guardrails

ControlImplementation
No citation, no claimNumeric sentences without a resolvable citation are struck before render, with a visible count of what was withheld and a drawer to inspect it.
FACT / INFERENCE separationDistinct chrome, not distinct tone. Facts render only from deterministic pipelines. Marked at glyph level, not by background tint — users habituate to background colour within a week, and it survives neither screenshot nor clipboard.
Confidence and decay surfacedEvery claim shows its score; expired claims are struck and flagged rather than quietly dropped.
Untrusted content boundaryFilings, news and commentary are wrapped and declared as data. Instructions inside them are never followed, and an attempt is reported.
Model-deprecation runbookModel ids pinned in config, an inventory of every behaviour that is model-specific, and a re-eval gate before any swap. No single model's behaviour is a load-bearing security invariant.
Refusal handlingA declined request is surfaced honestly rather than retried into a worse answer.
